Characteristic and Outcomes of Patients With ST-Segment-Elevation Myocardial Infarction Who Have Nonsystem Reasons for Delay in Treatment: A Report from the American Heart Association GWTG-CAD Registry.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-11-22 · Registry analysis (GWTG-CAD)

Lee TC, Jollis JG, Hong H, Jiang T, Norland K, Zhao J, Henry TD, Jacobs AK, et al.

Objective. Describe characteristics and in-hospital outcomes of STEMI patients with registry-recorded nonsystem reasons for delay (NSD) and evaluate the impact of exception practices on hospital performance rankings.

Key results. Among 74,372 primary-PCI STEMI patients, 23.9% had NSD; NSD was associated with higher in-hospital mortality (15.4% vs 2.7%; adjusted OR 2.61), driven largely by critical NSD (adjusted OR 4.61) though noncritical NSD also conferred excess risk (adjusted OR 1.57). Exception of all NSD caused substantial hospital ranking instability that improved when only critical NSD were excepted.

Clinical relevance. Highlights that NSD exceptions are common and linked to worse outcomes and that current exception practices can distort hospital performance metrics.

Why this paper matters. Important for clinicians and quality leads assessing door-to-balloon performance and for policy-makers refining exception criteria to better reflect true care quality.

External validation of an artificial intelligence-based ECG algorithm for the early diagnosis of acute myocardial infarction: a prospective diagnostic cohort study.

The Lancet regional health. Europe · 2026-11-07 · Prospective diagnostic cohort (external validation)

Champetier A, Lee MS, Lopez-Ayala P, Jo YY, Boeddinghaus J, Koechlin L, Bima P, Crisanti L, et al.

Objective. Externally validate an AI-ECG algorithm for early diagnosis/triage of acute myocardial infarction in European emergency department cohorts and compare incremental value to hs-cTnT.

Key results. In two European cohorts (n≈12,630), AI-ECG achieved AUCs of 0.78–0.84 for AMI and high sensitivity at predefined rule-out thresholds; performance was inferior to hs-cTnT but added incremental diagnostic value when combined with hs-cTnT.

Clinical relevance. AI-ECG alone is insufficient to rule out AMI but may augment early triage when combined with hs-cTnT; could inform ED workflows pending prospective outcome studies.

Why this paper matters. Provides important external validation across populations and supports further prospective studies of combined AI-ECG+troponin strategies for rapid ED decision-making.

Comparative Accuracy of the European Society of Cardiology Rapid Rule-Out and Rule-In Protocols for Diagnosing Non-ST-Segment-Elevation Myocardial Infarction: A Network Meta-Analysis.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-11-18 · Network meta-analysis of diagnostic accuracy studies

Ohtake H, Terasawa T, Zhelev Z, Rogers M, Peters JL, Restan IZ, Thulin VIL, Aakre KM, et al.

Objective. Compare accuracy of ESC rapid 0/1h, 0/2h, and 0/3h hs-cTn algorithms across assays for diagnosing NSTEMI and identify any superior hs-cTn assay.

Key results. Across included studies, the ESC 0/1-hour algorithm showed uniformly high sensitivity (≈97.8%–99.8%) and good specificity, with no clear superiority of any particular hs-cTn assay; evidence for 0/2 and 0/3-hour algorithms was limited.

Clinical relevance. Supports the diagnostic reliability of the ESC 0/1-hour algorithm across assays and suggests assay selection may be guided by practical considerations rather than clear performance differences.

Why this paper matters. Reassures clinicians and health systems that 0/1-hour protocols are broadly robust, but continued assay validation is warranted.

Exercise Dose during Cardiac Rehabilitation and Long-Term Mortality: Beyond Program Attendance.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-11-18 · Retrospective cohort (exercise dose quantification)

Marshall PW, Douglas S, Zhiyuan X, Collins BEG, Benatar J, Reading S, Kingsley MIC

Objective. Examine the association between objectively quantified supervised exercise dose during outpatient cardiac rehabilitation and long-term mortality and 12-month readmission.

Key results. Among 1,032 patients (mean follow-up ~6.9 years), higher achieved weekly exercise dose during rehabilitation was independently associated with lower long-term mortality (clear separation across dose quartiles), though association was attenuated after adjusting for baseline functional capacity; no association with 12-month readmission.

Clinical relevance. Quantified exercise exposure during supervised rehab predicts long-term survival beyond mere attendance metrics and could be used to refine program targets and counselling.

Why this paper matters. Provides actionable evidence that actual exercise dose, not just attendance, matters for long-term outcomes and supports dose-focused rehabilitation metrics.

Temporal Trends in Antiplatelet Strategies After PCI: A Nationwide Study by Presentation and Bleeding Risk.

JACC. Asia · 2026-10-31 · Nationwide claims-based temporal trend study

Lee K, Kim J, Park S, Lee H, Pack D, Lee S, Park H, Woo Y, et al.

Objective. Characterize temporal trends in antiplatelet strategies after PCI (by presentation and bleeding risk) and associated outcomes between 2014–2021.

Key results. Extended DAPT rose early (2014–2016) then declined (2016–2021); potent P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy remained uncommon; 1-year major bleeding declined from 2.6% to 2.0%, while trends in major adverse cardiac events were heterogeneous across subgroups.

Clinical relevance. Real-world antiplatelet practice partially reflects evolving evidence but remains heterogeneous, with a reassuring decline in major bleeding at population level.

Why this paper matters. Useful for clinicians and policymakers monitoring uptake of risk-tailored antiplatelet strategies and their population-level safety impacts.

Temporal and Vessel-Specific Relationship Between Post-PCI Fractional Flow Reserve and Long-Term Clinical Outcomes.

Circulation. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026-11-17 · Observational registry analysis (Post-PCI FFR Extended registry)

Kanaji Y, Hwang D, Usui E, Ueno H, Sayama K, Watanabe T, Watanabe T, Murakami R, et al.

Objective. Assess vessel-specific relationships between post-PCI fractional flow reserve (FFR) and 5-year target vessel failure and examine temporal stability of prognostic value.

Key results. Lower post-PCI FFR independently predicted higher 5-year target vessel failure in both LAD and non-LAD vessels; exploratory vessel-specific cut points (LAD 0.83, non-LAD 0.94) were proposed and prognostic discrimination persisted over 5 years with most excess risk in the first 3 years.

Clinical relevance. Supports the prognostic utility of post-PCI FFR for longer-term risk stratification and suggests considering vessel-specific interpretation to guide follow-up or further optimization.

Why this paper matters. May influence postprocedural physiologic assessment practices and targeted secondary prevention/follow-up strategies, though proposed thresholds need external validation.
