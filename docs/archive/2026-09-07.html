Intake of high-intensity statin after acute myocardial infarction assessed by direct drug concentration measurement: association with LDL-C response and coronary plaque modifications in the PACMAN-AMI trial.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 04 · Pre-specified sub-study of randomized trial (PACMAN-AMI) with biochemical adherence assessment

van der Velpen V, Häner JD, Kakizaki R, Bär S, Ueki Y, Siontis GCM, Stortecky S, Biccirè FG, et al.

Objective. To assess adherence to protocol-mandated high-intensity statin therapy after AMI using direct plasma drug measurements and examine associations with LDL-C response and intracoronary plaque imaging changes.

Key results. Among 255 patients with imaging follow-up, non-adherence was rare (1.2% at 4 weeks, 2.0% at 52 weeks); adherent patients had greater LDL-C reduction and more favourable plaque stabilization metrics on multimodality imaging.

Clinical relevance. Confirms high adherence within this trial cohort and supports the internal validity of PACMAN-AMI plaque outcome findings; highlights feasibility of direct drug-level adherence checks in trials.

Why this paper matters. Demonstrates that measured adherence was excellent and that imaging/biochemical treatment effects are unlikely to be driven by non-adherence in this study population.

The ACS EuroPath survey series: time trends in lipid management after an acute coronary syndrome.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 04 · Multicountry survey series (ACS EuroPath)

Laufs U, De Caterina R, Schiele F, Sionis A, Zaman A, Catapano AL, ACS EuroPath Trained Group

Objective. To evaluate temporal trends (2018–2024) in lipid testing and management practices among cardiologists treating ACS patients and impact of targeted training on guideline implementation.

Key results. Between 2018 and 2024, LDL-C testing remained high while use of combination therapy (statin+ezetimibe, PCSK9i, bempedoic acid) increased and LDL-C goal attainment improved (14%→31%→42%); trained cardiologists showed better adherence to guideline testing and therapy.

Clinical relevance. Documents real-world improvements in post-ACS lipid management and the positive effect of clinician training on achieving LDL-C targets in secondary prevention after ACS.

Why this paper matters. Highlights progress but persistent gaps in LDL-C goal attainment post-ACS and supports targeted education and system-level interventions to further improve secondary prevention.

The presence or absence of standard modifiable cardiovascular risk factors in patients with myocardial infarction impacts long-term but not 30-day mortality: a UK Biobank prospective cohort study.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 04 · Prospective cohort (UK Biobank)

Wong WK, Takeuchi F, Kuan LL, Nicholls SJ, Peter K, Chew DP

Objective. To determine whether absence of standard modifiable cardiovascular risk factors (SMuRFs) is associated with increased 30-day mortality after incident myocardial infarction.

Key results. Among 15,463 MI cases (1,034 without SMuRFs), SMuRF-absence was not associated with higher 30-day mortality (adjusted HR 0.82, P=0.103); however, SMuRF-absence was associated with lower 10-year mortality (OR 0.61).

Clinical relevance. Contradicts prior reports of higher short-term mortality in SMuRF-less MI patients and suggests early survival is driven by acute management rather than baseline risk factor profile.

Why this paper matters. Clarifies prognosis of SMuRF-less MI patients, informing risk communication and focus on long-term secondary prevention strategies.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 04 · Randomized controlled trial secondary analysis (HUYGENS) with serial OCT imaging

Di Giovanni G, Fujino M, Kataoka Y, Butters J, Hucko T, Puri R, Nissen SE, Nelson AJ, et al.

Objective. To evaluate whether baseline lipoprotein(a) modifies evolocumab's effects on plaque phenotype changes over 50 weeks in patients after ACS.

Key results. Role of Social Determinants of Health in Risk Prediction Models for ST-Segment-Elevation Myocardial Infarction Death: A Registry-Based Study.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-20 · Registry-based predictive modelling (GWTG-CAD)

Pliakos EE, Shultz K, Reddy KP, Eberly LA, Khatana SAM, Fanaroff AC, Groeneveld PW, Giri J, et al.

Objective. To evaluate whether adding zip code–level social determinants of health (SDOH) improves risk prediction of in-hospital death after STEMI over a high-performing clinical model.

Key results. In 749,410 STEMI admissions, adding SDOH yielded no meaningful improvement in discrimination over the clinical model (both had excellent discrimination); a few SDOH variables gave modest statistical gains but no clinically relevant improvement.

Clinical relevance. Suggests short-term in-hospital STEMI mortality prediction is dominated by acute clinical variables and that zip code–level SDOH add limited incremental predictive value in this setting.

Why this paper matters. Guides modelers and clinicians that routine inclusion of area-level SDOH may not materially improve in-hospital STEMI death prediction, although SDOH remain important for broader disparities research and post-discharge outcomes.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-20 · Prespecified subgroup analysis of randomized controlled trial (OPT-BIRISK)

Zhang D, Li Y, Qiu M, Zhou Y, Chen S, Pei H, Liu J, Xu B, et al.

Objective. To assess whether clopidogrel monotherapy (after 9–12 months of DAPT) reduces bleeding without increasing ischemic events in birisk ACS patients with diabetes.

Key results. In 4,072 diabetic birisk patients, clopidogrel monotherapy reduced BARC 2/3/5 bleeding (2.1% vs 3.2%; HR 0.66) with no increase in major adverse cardiac and cerebral events (2.9% vs 3.6%; HR 0.79) compared with continuation of clopidogrel+aspirin.

Clinical relevance. Supports de-escalation to clopidogrel monotherapy for 9 months in birisk ACS patients with diabetes to reduce bleeding while maintaining ischemic safety after initial DAPT period.

Why this paper matters. Provides randomized evidence to tailor prolonged antiplatelet strategies in a high bleeding/ischemic risk subgroup (including diabetics) following PCI for ACS.

Sundermeyer J, Li S, Ton VK, Kataria R, Zweck E, Garan AR, Kanwar MK, Hernandez-Montfort J, et al.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-20 · Prospective diagnostic multicenter study with external validation

Koechlin L, Boeddinghaus J, Lopez-Ayala P, Jacobsen G, McCord J, Heimbach B, Nestelberger T, Wildi K, et al.

Objective. To compare the ESC 0/1h and 0/2h high-sensitivity troponin (hs-cTn) algorithms’ safety and efficacy for rule-in/-out of MI in patients with prior CABG.

Key results. In 356 patients with prior CABG, both algorithms maintained high safety; the 0/2h algorithm had higher efficacy (58.4% vs 50.6%) with comparable sensitivity/specificity across assays and external validation cohort.

Clinical relevance. In patients with prior CABG presenting with chest pain, the 0/2h hs-cTn algorithm may triage a greater proportion of patients safely compared with the 0/1h algorithm.

Why this paper matters. Guides emergency department diagnostic strategy for faster and more efficient MI rule-in/-out in a high-risk subgroup with prior CABG.

Noncoding RNAs as Prognostic Biomarkers After Myocardial Infarction: A Scoping Review.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-20 · Scoping review

Wi Niowski Ł, Biskupski M, Błaszczyk R, Kondracki B, Siwiec J, Mlak R, Głowniak A

Objective. To map human studies of circulating noncoding RNAs measured after myocardial infarction and their associations with prognostic phenotypes (remodeling, no-reflow, clinical events, treatment response).

Key results. Forty-six heterogeneous studies identified candidate microRNAs and long noncoding RNAs linked to no-reflow, remodeling, clinical events and treatment response; most signals remain phenotype specific and lack standardization or external validation.

Clinical relevance. Noncoding RNAs show promise as adjunctive post-MI biomarkers but current evidence is preliminary and requires standardized sampling, assay methods and validation before clinical use.

Why this paper matters. Highlights potential biomarker avenues to refine post-MI risk stratification and personalize secondary prevention, while underscoring substantial translational gaps.

Triglyceride reduction after MI and major adverse outcomes in SWEDEHEART-insights for future trials.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 04 · Observational cohort (SWEDEHEART registry)

Schubert J, Hagström E, Westerbergh J, Leosdottir M, Ray K

Objective. To assess the association between triglyceride change from admission to 1 year after MI and subsequent cardiovascular outcomes, and to inform trial design for triglyceride-lowering therapies.

Key results. In 51,719 MI patients, large triglyceride reductions (top quartile, median reduction ~1.0 mmol/L from baseline ~2.2 mmol/L) were associated with lower MACE (HR 0.85), all-cause mortality (HR 0.90) and non-fatal MI (HR 0.83); patients with 1-year triglycerides <0.9 mmol/L had lowest risk.

Clinical relevance. Identifies patient phenotype (baseline TG ≳2.2 mmol/L) and magnitude of reduction (~1.0 mmol/L) that may be needed to observe clinical benefit, informing selection criteria for future triglyceride-lowering trials and secondary prevention strategies.

Why this paper matters. May explain prior neutral trials and suggests trials should enrol higher-triglyceride post-MI patients and target substantial TG reductions to detect outcome benefits.

Comparative efficacy of LDL-C-lowering therapies in first-time vs. recurrent myocardial infarction prevention: a meta-analysis of large-scale randomized controlled trials.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 04 · Systematic review and meta-analysis of large RCTs

Guo BQ, Li HB, Zhao B, Xu PW

Objective. To compare the efficacy of LDL-C-lowering therapies in preventing first-time versus recurrent myocardial infarction using large randomized controlled trials.

Key results. Pooled analysis of 22 RCTs (n≈180,304) showed a 38% MI risk reduction for first-time MI prevention (RR 0.62) versus a 16% reduction for recurrent MI prevention (RR 0.84); difference between groups was statistically significant.

Clinical relevance. Suggests relative benefits of LDL-C lowering may be greater in primary prevention for first MI than for recurrent MI, with implications for risk-stratified treatment expectations in secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Helps set realistic expectations about absolute and relative benefit of LDL-C lowering in recurrent MI populations and reinforces continued optimization of therapies in secondary prevention.

Residual Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease Risk in Statin Users: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-20 · Systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies

Watanabe AH, Bash LD, Westley T, Garcia A, Aiello E, Bradbury J, Garg J, Telukuntla V, et al.

Objective. To quantify residual ASCVD event rates among statin users in routine practice across multiple outcomes (MACE/all-cause death, MI, stroke, cardiovascular death, revascularization).

Key results. Pooled residual composite risk was 12.3 events per 1000 person-years overall, higher in patients with prior ASCVD (15.8 PPY) and lower in at-risk adults (6.4 PPY); myocardial infarction pooled rate ~8.2 PPY and ischemic stroke ~6.0 PPY, with substantial between-study heterogeneity.

Clinical relevance. Documents meaningful residual ASCVD risk despite statin therapy in real-world populations, highlighting need for additional risk-reduction strategies and optimization of secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Quantifies the treatment gap remaining on statins and supports consideration of intensified, multi-modality secondary prevention for residual risk.
