Tropical cyclones and acute coronary syndromes: a Chinese nationwide study.

European heart journal · 2026 Sep 08 · Time-stratified case-crossover study (nationwide registry)

Li S, Wang W, Zhou H, Zhang Y, Chen L, Lin J, Nawsherwan, Huo Y, et al.

Objective. To evaluate the short-term association between tropical cyclone exposure and incidence of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) in China and identify effect modifiers.

Key results. Among 2,563,780 ACS patients, tropical cyclone days were associated with a 14% increased risk of ACS over the 0–3 day period (95% CI 2%–27%) and with longer pre-hospital and in-hospital delays; stronger effects in men, lower-education, and those with more risk factors.

Clinical relevance. Indicates environmental disaster exposure acutely increases ACS incidence and delays care, informing emergency preparedness and public health strategies to mitigate ACS burden during cyclones.

Why this paper matters. Directly relevant to ACS systems-of-care and public health planning; highlights a modifiable contextual risk for acute events and treatment delays.

Joint association of sedentary behaviour and physical activity with cardiovascular disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 08 · Systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies

Lunar FR, Du Z, Amagasa S, Kondo N, Inoue K

Objective. To synthesize evidence on the joint associations of physical activity (PA) and sedentary behaviour (SB) with cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk in adults.

Key results. Across 17 studies, the lowest PA + highest SB group had pooled HR 1.78 (95% CI 1.59–2.00) for CVD vs highest PA + lowest SB; intermediate combinations showed smaller but significant increases in risk.

Clinical relevance. Supports integrating both PA promotion and reduction of sedentary time into cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention counseling rather than addressing either behaviour alone.

Why this paper matters. Provides pooled evidence that joint consideration of PA and SB better stratifies CVD risk and can guide behavioral targets in rehabilitation programs.

Optimizing physical activity bouts to interrupt sedentary behaviour for cardiometabolic health: a systematic review and meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 08 · Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

Vanherle J, Franssen GHLM, Ivanova A, Eijnde BO, Franssen WMA

Objective. To determine which characteristics (frequency, duration, intensity) of physical activity bouts best improve cardiometabolic outcomes and interrupt sedentary behaviour.

Key results. Frequent PA bouts improved glucose regulation (SMD -0.22); longer/higher-intensity bouts reduced triglycerides (SMD -0.27); moderate-to-vigorous bouts improved endothelial function and lowered insulin and BP.

Clinical relevance. Provides evidence-based guidance on bout frequency, duration and intensity to optimize cardiometabolic benefits within rehabilitation programs and behavioral prescriptions.

Why this paper matters. Helps refine exercise prescriptions in cardiac rehabilitation by aligning bout characteristics with specific cardiometabolic goals.

Accelerometer-derived weekend warrior physical activity pattern and all-cause and cause-specific mortality in patients with cardiovascular disease.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 08 · Prospective cohort (accelerometer sub-study)

Wang L, Qiao Y, Yu Y, Zhao M, Yang L, Xi B

Objective. To examine whether a 'weekend warrior' pattern (≥150 min/week MVPA concentrated in 1–2 days) is associated with mortality among patients with established CVD.

Key results. Among 8,128 UK Biobank CVD patients, weekend warriors had lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.52–0.71) comparable to regularly active participants.

Clinical relevance. Suggests that concentrated weekly MVPA (weekend warrior pattern) may be an acceptable, pragmatic alternative activity pattern for CVD patients who struggle with distributed exercise during rehabilitation.

Why this paper matters. Supports flexible activity prescriptions in cardiac rehabilitation, potentially improving adherence without losing mortality benefits.

The proteomic profile of leisure time physical activity across two decades: implications for future cardiovascular risk and mortality.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 08 · Prospective cohort proteomics study

Enarsson M, Gustafsson S, Lampa E, Rudholm Feldreich T, Beigrezaei S, Butterworth AS, Elmståhl S, Engström G, et al.

Objective. To examine associations between 20-year leisure-time physical activity and plasma proteomic profiles, and whether these proteins predict imminent and long-term myocardial infarction and mortality.

Key results. Higher long-term leisure PA inversely associated with 12 plasma proteins (inflammatory, immune, metabolic); several of these proteins were linked to imminent MI, long-term MI, and mortality.

Clinical relevance. Provides mechanistic biomarker links between sustained physical activity and reduced cardiovascular events that may inform targets for risk monitoring in rehabilitation and prevention programs.

Why this paper matters. Helps explain biological pathways by which long-term physical activity confers cardioprotection, reinforcing sustained activity in secondary prevention.

Improvements in walking speed reduce mortality in patients with cardiovascular disease during exercise-based secondary prevention: findings from the ITER registry.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 08 · Multicenter prospective registry analysis (ITER registry)

Raisi A, Zerbini V, Myers J, Piva T, Menegatti E, Grazzi G, Mazzoni G, Mandini S

Objective. To evaluate associations between walking speed (1-km treadmill test) and mortality in patients with stable CVD and to assess how changes in walking speed over time during exercise-based secondary prevention relate to mortality.

Key results. In 2,858 patients, higher baseline walking speed and improvements over time were associated with substantially lower all-cause and CVD mortality over median 11 years; LowWS-improved group had ~38% lower all-cause mortality risk.

Clinical relevance. Supports use of walking speed as a simple, prognostic, and modifiable metric in exercise-based secondary prevention programs and for tracking functional response to cardiac rehabilitation.

Why this paper matters. Demonstrates that measurable improvements in functional capacity during rehabilitation translate into long-term mortality benefit, supporting functional targets in secondary prevention.

A remote booster program to attenuate sedentary behaviour in patients with coronary artery disease: a randomized controlled trial.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 08 · Randomized controlled trial (remote behavioral booster)

Kroesen SH, van Bakel BMA, de Bruin M, Pisters MF, Ortega FB, Hopman MTE, Thijssen DHJ, Bakker EA, et al.

Objective. To test whether a 3-week remote booster behavioral intervention reduces accelerometer-measured sedentary time in coronary artery disease patients who completed cardiac rehabilitation.

Key results. In 42 CAD patients randomized 1:1, the booster group had greater reductions in sedentary time (-1.3 h/day vs -0.1 h/day) and prolonged sedentary bouts, and larger increases in light physical activity compared with control.

Clinical relevance. Provides randomized evidence that a brief remote booster can reduce sedentary behaviour after completion of CR, offering a scalable strategy to sustain activity gains in secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Addresses the common post-rehabilitation relapse to sedentarism with a feasible remote intervention that could be integrated into long-term secondary prevention pathways.

Cross-Ancestry Proteogenomic Analyses Identified New Therapeutic Insights for Ischemic Heart Disease.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-07-29 · Proteogenomic Mendelian randomization study

Mazidi M, Wright N, Pozarickij A, Millwood IY, Walters R, Kartsonaki C, Bennett DA, Yao P, et al.

Objective. To use cis-pQTLs from East Asian and European cohorts to identify and validate plasma protein targets causally linked to ischemic heart disease (IHD) for potential therapeutic development beyond statins.

Key results. MR implicated 54 unique proteins with genetic support for IHD; colocalization confirmed 15 gene-protein-IHD associations (including multiple lipid- and inflammation-related proteins) with some cross-ancestry concordance and discordance.

Clinical relevance. Identifies and prioritizes protein targets (lipid- and inflammation-related) that could inform novel or repurposed therapies for IHD prevention and treatment beyond LDL lowering.

Why this paper matters. Translates large-scale proteomics and genetics into candidate therapeutic targets relevant to secondary prevention of ischemic events; useful for translational researchers and clinicians following evolving pharmacotherapies.

Monogenic ALB Variants as Determinants of Severe Hypercholesterolemia: A Population-Based Cohort Study.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-05-13 · Population-based cohort genetics study

Berry ASF, Cawley D, Hattenberger A, Chang AR, Hegele RA, Gidding SS, Oetjens MT

Objective. To assess the effect of ALB loss-of-function (LoF) variants and other causes of low albumin on LDL-C levels across two large biobanks.

Key results. 77 carriers of ALB LoF among ~561k participants showed ~0.69 g/dL lower albumin and a 38.3 mg/dL higher LDL-C compared with noncarriers; polygenic lower albumin had opposite, modest effects on LDL-C.

Clinical relevance. Describes a previously underrecognized monogenic cause of marked LDL-C elevation that can mimic familial hypercholesterolemia and may affect lipid-targeted secondary prevention strategies.

Why this paper matters. Highlights a genetic mechanism leading to severe hypercholesterolemia with direct implications for lipid screening, cascade testing, and individualized secondary prevention.

Prediction of incident heart failure in established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease: the SMART2-HF model.

European heart journal · 2026 Sep 08 · Model development and external validation cohort study

Reitsma TH, Lim CE, Gynnild MN, Kaptoge S, Lõo L, Holtrop J, Kuźma Ł, Pennells L, et al.

Objective. To develop and externally validate SMART2-HF, a model predicting 10-year and lifetime incident heart failure (HF) in patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD).

Key results. Developed in 7,698 patients and externally validated in 240,741 across six data sources with pooled C-statistic 0.696 (95% CI 0.674–0.717); predicted risks matched observed incidence.

Clinical relevance. Offers a validated risk tool to identify ASCVD patients at higher risk for incident HF to inform surveillance and secondary prevention strategies.

Why this paper matters. Provides clinicians a complementary, externally validated model to personalize HF risk assessment in secondary prevention populations.

Polygenic Risk Based Detection and Treatment of Subclinical Coronary Atherosclerosis in the PROACT Clinical Trials.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-02-18 · Feasibility/implementation report from genotype-first clinical trials

Abou-Karam R, Kim MS, Jemma Cho SM, Bitar F, Gady S, Cheng F, Thompson AG, Karlson EW, et al.

Objective. To report feasibility and early implementation outcomes of PROACT, a genotype-first trial identifying individuals with high coronary artery disease (CAD) PRS for subclinical plaque detection and preventive pharmacotherapy.

Key results. From 64,092 genotyped participants, 2,495 (3.9%) were eligible with high CAD PRS despite low clinical risk; of first 204 imaged, 50% had subclinical plaque, with higher prevalence in men (76%).

Clinical relevance. Demonstrates feasibility of genotype-first recruitment and high yield of subclinical atherosclerosis among genetically high-risk but clinically low-risk individuals who may benefit from early secondary prevention measures.

Why this paper matters. Illustrates a potential precision-prevention pathway to identify 'silent' high-risk patients for earlier intervention beyond routine risk scoring.

Refined physical activity recommendations considering cardiovascular disease risk stratification: a cohort study.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 08 · Prospective cohort (UK Biobank, accelerometer-derived activity)

Yuan C, Zhou T, Cui X, Xie S, Lu X, Liu F

Objective. To identify optimized physical activity patterns across SCORE2-predicted CVD risk strata and refine PA recommendations accordingly.

Key results. Inverse association between MVPA and incident CVD was strongest in very-high-risk individuals (20% lower hazard per 150 min/week MVPA); suggested VPA upper thresholds (60 min/week high-risk, 42 min/week very high-risk) beyond which benefits may diminish.

Clinical relevance. Supports tailoring MVPA/VPA targets to baseline CVD risk when counseling patients in secondary prevention to maximize benefit and limit potential harm from excessive vigorous exercise.

Why this paper matters. Adds evidence to personalize exercise prescriptions in secondary prevention and cardiac rehabilitation according to patient risk profile.

Self-rated physical fitness predicts cardiovascular and all-cause mortality-implications for clinical decision-making.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 08 · Prospective cohort (LURIC study)

Moissl AP, Delgado GE, Kleber ME, Mooren FC, Schäfer H, Krämer BK, März W, Schmitz B

Objective. To assess whether self-rated physical fitness (SRPF) predicts cardiovascular and all-cause mortality in a cohort with and without angiographic CAD.

Key results. In 3,248 participants over ~10 years, higher SRPF was strongly associated with lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality (highest vs lowest SRPF class HR for CV mortality 0.14) independent of traditional risk factors and comorbidities.

Clinical relevance. SRPF is a simple, low-cost prognostic measure that could be used in clinics to aid risk stratification and motivate interventions during secondary prevention follow-up.

Why this paper matters. Offers an easily obtainable patient-reported metric that correlates with hard outcomes and can complement objective assessments in secondary prevention.
