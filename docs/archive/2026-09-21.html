Landi A, Malik FTN, Chieffo A, Valgimigli M

Ultrashort vs Standard-Duration Dual Antiplatelet Therapy in Acute Coronary Syndrome Patients Undergoing PCI: A Meta-Analysis.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026 Sep 14 · Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Hendrianus H, Gorog DA, Tantry US, Navarese EP, Lee SY, Cho JH, Jo J, Bae J, et al.

Objective. Compare ultrashort (≤1-month) versus standard-duration DAPT in ACS patients undergoing PCI for efficacy and safety.

Key results. Across 10 trials (n=29,232) ultrashort DAPT did not increase overall MACCE (HR 1.06) while reducing major bleeding (HR 0.47) and improving net clinical outcome (HR 0.83); signals of increased MACCE were present in STEMI and with very intensive abbreviation strategies (≤1 week or clopidogrel/aspirin monotherapy).

Clinical relevance. Supports consideration of abbreviated DAPT in selected ACS patients to lower bleeding, but cautions against ultrashort/very intensive strategies in STEMI patients.

Why this paper matters. Directly informs post-PCI antiplatelet duration decisions in ACS and highlights subgroups (STEMI, abbreviation intensity, East Asian ethnicity) where risks differ.

Accelerated Diagnostic Pathways for Suspected Acute Coronary Syndrome in Practice: A Randomized Trial of 0/1-Hour vs 0/3-Hour Troponin Testing.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-05-20 · Pragmatic Randomized Trial

Hatherley J, Dakshi A, Collinson P, Miller G, Bailey L, Davies S, Fearon H, Meah N, et al.

Objective. Compare efficiency and safety of ESC 0/1-hour versus 0/3-hour hs-cTn accelerated diagnostic pathways in suspected ACS in real-world practice.

Key results. In 3,543 patients the 0/1-hour pathway was noninferior for safety (sensitivity for MACE) but did not significantly increase proportion discharged within 4 hours (21.8% vs 19.2%); calculated sensitivities were 93.7% vs 89.5% respectively.

Clinical relevance. Real-world implementation of 0/1-hour troponin pathway is safe but may not deliver expected ED efficiency gains due to laboratory turnaround and system constraints.

Why this paper matters. Helps set realistic expectations for ED throughput when adopting rapid troponin algorithms and underscores system factors limiting discharge efficiency.

Bivalirudin Versus Heparin in Low and Non-Low Bleeding Risk Patients Undergoing Primary PCI for STEMI: The BRIGHT-4 Trial.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-08-28 · Randomized Controlled Trial (prespecified subgroup analysis)

Qiu M, Duan Y, Feng X, Chen K, Zhang D, Zou Y, Zheng X, Zhao G, et al.

Objective. Assess whether benefits of bivalirudin versus heparin in primary PCI for STEMI apply across bleeding-risk strata (CRUSADE score <30 vs ≥30).

Key results. Among 6,016 STEMI patients, bivalirudin with post-PCI infusion reduced 30-day composite of death or major bleeding overall; benefit in reducing the primary outcome was seen in both low- and higher-bleeding-risk groups, with similar effects on mortality.

Clinical relevance. Supports use of bivalirudin plus short post-PCI infusion as an anticoagulation option in radial-access primary PCI across bleeding-risk categories.

Why this paper matters. Provides randomized evidence to inform anticoagulant choice in primary PCI, particularly when bleeding risk stratification is considered.

Intravenous Nicorandil in Patients With ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction Undergoing Primary PCI: The CLEAN Randomized Clinical Trial.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-08-12 · Randomized Controlled Trial

Huang D, Wu H, Lu Y, Liu Q, Cai H, Shen J, Ma L, Yu Z, et al.

Objective. Determine whether adjunctive intravenous nicorandil reduces 12-month composite cardiovascular outcomes in STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI.

Key results. In 1,503 patients there was no significant difference in the 12-month primary composite outcome (13.1% nicorandil vs 13.1% placebo); secondary endpoints showed nominal reductions in cardiovascular death and target-vessel revascularization but overall no primary benefit.

Clinical relevance. Does not support routine use of intravenous nicorandil as adjunctive therapy in unselected STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI.

Why this paper matters. Informs practice by discouraging routine adjunctive nicorandil for long-term outcome improvement after primary PCI given neutral primary results.

Fractional Flow Reserve in Acute Coronary Syndrome: An Updated Systematic Review and Bayesian Meta-Analysis.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-09 · Systematic Review and Bayesian Meta-Analysis

Ha ET, Shah H, Zhang C, Phichaphop A, Waksman O, Verma B, Kobayashi Y, Waksman R, et al.

Objective. Evaluate performance of FFR-guided revascularization in the setting of acute coronary syndrome compared with chronic coronary syndrome and with angiography-guided strategies in ACS.

Key results. Among 11 trials (13,029 patients), FFR-guided lesions in ACS were associated with higher 1-year MACE risk compared with FFR in chronic coronary syndrome (RD 1.9%); in ACS patients there was no difference in 1-year MACE between FFR-guided and angiography-guided revascularization.

Clinical relevance. Suggests FFR-directed physiology may be less reliable or associated with higher residual risk in ACS than in stable disease, and does not clearly outperform angiography-guided strategies for 1-year outcomes in ACS.

Why this paper matters. Highlights limitations of physiology-guided deferral/decision-making in the acute setting and the need for further research optimizing FFR use in ACS.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-07-15 · Review

Galli M, Capodanno D, Berg JT, Cavallari LH, Rao SV, Lemos PA, Valgimigli M, Gaudino M, et al.

Objective. Review evidence and practical considerations for aspirin withdrawal strategies in patients with coronary artery disease to reduce bleeding without compromising ischemic protection.

Key results. Summarizes trial data showing variable efficacy/safety of aspirin withdrawal depending on clinical context, timing after ACS/PCI, patient risk profile, concomitant OAC use, and choice of P2Y12 inhibitor; provides guidance for individualized implementation.

Clinical relevance. Helps clinicians weigh trade-offs of early aspirin discontinuation after ACS/PCI, particularly in patients at high bleeding risk or on oral anticoagulation.

Why this paper matters. Offers practical, evidence-based recommendations to tailor antithrombotic regimens and reduce bleeding while preserving ischemic protection in secondary prevention.

Effect of Semaglutide on the Inflammatory Biomarker High-Sensitivity CRP in Patients With Established Cardiovascular Disease and Overweight or Obesity in SELECT: A Prespecified Secondary Analysis.

Circulation · 2026-08-18 · Prespecified Secondary Analysis of a Phase III RCT (SELECT)

Plutzky J, Bogdański P, Colhoun HM, Dağdelen S, Deanfield JE, Emerson SS, Hovingh GK, Kahn SE, et al.

Objective. Assess baseline and treatment-related changes in hsCRP with semaglutide and their relation to MACE in patients with established ASCVD and overweight/obesity (without diabetes).

Key results. Baseline hsCRP predicted MACE risk and semaglutide reduced hsCRP by ~38% at 104 weeks; hsCRP reductions were associated with greater weight loss but appeared early and partially independent of weight change, and reductions correlated with lower MACE risk.

Clinical relevance. Suggests anti-inflammatory effects of semaglutide may contribute to MACE reduction in secondary prevention among patients with ASCVD and obesity.

Why this paper matters. Supports consideration of GLP-1RA therapy for cardio-metabolic risk reduction beyond glycemic control and highlights inflammation as a modifiable marker in secondary prevention.

Clopidogrel vs Aspirin According to Diabetes Mellitus: A Prespecified Analysis of the SMART-CHOICE 3 Trial.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026-07-16 · Prespecified Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trial

Lee SY, Joo D, Lee JM, Park YH, Lee JY, Jeong JO, Kim CJ, Lee HC, et al.

Objective. Compare clopidogrel versus aspirin monotherapy after standard DAPT in patients (including a prespecified diabetes subgroup) post-PCI.

Key results. In 2,089 patients with diabetes, clopidogrel monotherapy vs aspirin was associated with lower MACCE (4.5% vs 9.1%; HR 0.57) without increased bleeding; overall interaction with diabetes was not statistically significant.

Clinical relevance. Supports clopidogrel as a reasonable monotherapy option after DAPT in post-PCI patients with diabetes to reduce ischemic events without excess bleeding.

Why this paper matters. Provides evidence to guide selection of antiplatelet monotherapy for secondary prevention after PCI in high-risk subgroups such as patients with diabetes.
