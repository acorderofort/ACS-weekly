Pathology of Genetic Variants in Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection.

Circulation · 2026-09-01 · Autopsy histopathology with whole-exome sequencing

Tanaka T, Kawakami R, Gaynor BJ, Williams D, Augenstreich J, Sakamoto A, Jinnouchi H, Kawai K, et al.

Objective. To characterize histopathologic differences in SCAD culprit lesions and assess the impact of pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in candidate SCAD genes on lesion morphology and protein expression.

Key results. Culprit SCAD lesions had reduced medial collagen and decreased smoothelin staining with smooth muscle cell phenotypic changes; pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants were found in 25% of cases and were associated with more frequent multivessel dissection and reduced protein expression for COL3A1, FBN1, or FLNA.

Clinical relevance. Provides pathological and genetic correlates that may inform risk stratification and future diagnostic approaches for SCAD-associated myocardial infarction, but does not alter acute management.

Why this paper matters. SCAD is an important non-atherosclerotic cause of ACS in certain populations; identifying structural and genetic vulnerabilities can guide research into targeted surveillance and therapies.

Metrnl Reduces Thrombosis and Acute Myocardial Injury Through Inhibition of Platelet Activation via CD36 Receptor Degradation.

Circulation · 2026-08-18 · Preclinical mechanistic study (mouse models, human/rodent platelets)

Miao ZW, Chen CX, Wang P, Zheng SL, Jiang JB, Hu WJ, Zhu XY, Lu B, et al.

Objective. To determine whether Metrnl modulates platelet activation and thrombosis and to elucidate the receptor/mechanism, and to test effects on myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury.

Key results. Metrnl inhibited platelet activation and thrombosis without increasing bleeding, acted via binding to CD36 and promoting CD36 degradation (reducing SRC–ERK5 signaling), and reduced microvascular thrombosis and myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury in mouse models.

Clinical relevance. Identifies Metrnl–CD36 signaling as a potential novel antithrombotic target relevant to acute MI and reperfusion injury; findings are preclinical and require clinical translation.

Why this paper matters. If validated in humans, targeting this pathway could provide new therapies to limit thrombosis and reperfusion injury in ACS beyond current antiplatelet strategies.

Great debate: preventive percutaneous coronary intervention added to optimal medical treatment should be the default treatment for non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 05 · Narrative review / debate (clinical review of imaging and preventive PCI evidence)

Park DW, Boden WE, De Caterina R, Gallinoro E, Ahn JM, Bairey Merz CN, Kaski JC, Kang DY, et al.

Objective. To examine arguments for and against preventive PCI of non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques identified by intracoronary imaging versus optimal medical therapy.

Key results. Advanced intracoronary imaging can identify high-risk plaques and the PREVENT trial showed reductions in a composite driven by softer endpoints; however, benefits on hard outcomes (mortality, MI) are unproven and concerns remain about procedural risks, cost-effectiveness, and interactions with modern intensive medical therapy.

Clinical relevance. Summarizes current evidence and uncertainties clinicians should consider when weighing preventive PCI as a secondary prevention strategy; supports cautious interpretation and need for further RCT data on hard endpoints.

Why this paper matters. This debate frames a potential paradigm shift in secondary prevention (plaque sealing) but highlights that evidence is currently insufficient to adopt preventive PCI as default care.

The benefit of LDL-C levels below 55 mg/dL in ESKD patients under statin treatment: A target trial emulation study.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-08-25 · Observational target-trial emulation (hemodialysis ESKD cohort)

Yen CL, Fan PC, Chen JJ, Lee CC, Ho WY, Tu YR, Chu PH, Hsiao CC, et al.

Objective. To assess whether achieving LDL-C <55 mg/dL in statin-treated ESKD patients is associated with lower cardiovascular mortality compared with higher LDL-C levels.

Key results. In a statin-treated ESKD cohort, LDL-C <55 mg/dL was associated with lower cardiovascular death versus LDL-C ≥100 mg/dL (HR 0.71; 95% CI 0.53–0.95); a non-significant trend toward lower all-cause mortality was observed.

Clinical relevance. Suggests that very low achieved LDL-C may be linked to reduced cardiovascular mortality in dialysis patients, challenging prior assumptions, but residual confounding limits causal inference and randomized data are needed.

Why this paper matters. ESKD patients have unclear benefit from lipid-lowering; these findings support further investigation of intensive LDL targets in this high-risk group for secondary prevention.

Discordant fatty acid profile drives residual inflammatory risk in metabolically vulnerable patients with low atherogenic lipoprotein burden.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-08-24 · Multi-cohort observational study with metabolomic phenotyping, OCT plaque imaging, and UK Biobank validation

Wang Y, Han C, Yao J, Feng Y, Qiao S, Liu C, Liu Z, Ren F, et al.

Objective. To determine whether latent fatty-acid phenotypes predict structural plaque vulnerability and incident MACE independent of atherogenic lipoprotein burden.

Key results. An omega-3–depleted phenotype (higher omega-6/omega-3 ratio) was associated with thinner fibrous caps and higher prevalence of thin-cap fibroatheroma on OCT, and with increased MACE risk in the UK Biobank (adjusted HR ~1.12), with much of the excess risk mediated via glycemic, inflammatory, and adiposity pathways.

Clinical relevance. Identifies a circulating fatty-acid phenotype that may mark residual inflammatory/metabolic risk despite low LDL and could help target non–lipid-modifying secondary prevention strategies; prospective validation is required.

Why this paper matters. Highlights mechanisms of residual risk beyond LDL and supports consideration of tailored interventions (e.g., anti-inflammatory, metabolic, or omega-3 strategies) to reduce recurrent events in high-risk patients.
