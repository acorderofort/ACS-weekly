Rapid Inflation-Deflation vs Pressure-Optimized Stenting During Primary PCI: The RAPID-POP Trial.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026 Aug 24 · Randomized controlled trial (multicenter)

Hakeem A, Ishaq H, Ali A, Memon FF, Shah JA, Ahmed I, Mengal MN, Lakho AA, et al.

Objective. Compare pressure‑optimized stent inflation (POP) versus conventional rapid inflation‑deflation stenting during primary PCI for STEMI to improve microvascular reperfusion.

Key results. POP improved hierarchical reperfusion outcomes (WIN ratio 1.42) with higher rates of ST‑segment resolution and TIMI 3 flow and reduced need for postdilation; trend toward fewer 30‑day MACE but not statistically significant.

Clinical relevance. Provides randomized evidence that an inflation protocol can improve immediate reperfusion quality during primary PCI and may be a simple, implementable procedural modification.

Why this paper matters. Directly relevant to STEMI care—an actionable technique that could improve microvascular reperfusion and deserves attention and potential incorporation into practice pending confirmatory larger trials.

Cardiac rehabilitation against frailty and sarcopenia in older individuals: a literature review.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Aug 27 · Review

Fukumoto Y

Objective. Review the relationship between frailty/sarcopenia and cardiovascular disease and evaluate cardiac rehabilitation (CR) interventions to counteract these syndromes in older patients.

Key results. Resistance training consistently improves muscle strength and function; conventional CR may need adaptation for frail patients, with options such as bodyweight resistance training or electrical muscle stimulation showing promise.

Clinical relevance. Provides practical strategies to tailor CR for frail or sarcopenic older patients to improve functional outcomes and reduce hospitalizations.

Why this paper matters. Frailty and sarcopenia worsen prognosis after cardiac events; adapting CR increases access and effectiveness in this growing patient group.

Cardiovascular risk management in the elderly: a dilemma in daily clinical practice.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Aug 27 · Review

Dacquino GM, Galati G, Genovese L, Bravo FF, Bernardi M, Spadafora L, Pedretti RFE

Objective. Summarize evidence and guideline recommendations for cardiovascular risk management in older adults, addressing hypertension, dyslipidaemia, diabetes, obesity, and antithrombotic therapy with attention to frailty and multimorbidity.

Key results. Describes adoption of SCORE2-OP risk tool and highlights benefits of therapies such as SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP‑1 receptor agonists; emphasizes individualized care given polypharmacy and frailty considerations.

Clinical relevance. Useful overview to inform secondary prevention decisions in older patients with established or high-risk CVD, helping tailor therapy and risk stratification.

Why this paper matters. Older adults are underrepresented in trials yet carry high CVD burden; this review consolidates contemporary guidance to reduce undertreatment or inappropriate de-escalation based on age.

Lipid-lowering therapy for cardiovascular risk management in the older patients: how far should we go?

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Aug 27 · Review

Tanaka T, Fukumoto Y

Objective. Assess the evidence for lipid‑lowering therapy in older adults, including statins and ezetimibe, and discuss how far LDL‑C lowering should be pursued with advancing age.

Key results. Cites PROSPER and EWTOPIA trials and meta-analyses showing meaningful reductions in coronary events with statins/ezetimibe in older adults, while noting diminishing absolute benefits in very advanced age and the need for individualized decisions.

Clinical relevance. Supports continuing or initiating lipid-lowering therapy in many older patients with or at high risk for ASCVD rather than withholding therapy solely due to age.

Why this paper matters. Addresses clinician uncertainty about lipid therapy in the elderly and reinforces that age alone is not a contraindication to secondary prevention with statins.

Global Burden of Elevated LDL-C: Findings From the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023.

JAMA · 2026 Aug 25 · Systematic review / Global burden analysis

GBD 2023 LDL Cholesterol Collaborators, Razo C, DeCleene NK, Johnson CO, Stark BA, LeGrand K, Aalruz H, Abaraogu UO, et al.

Objective. Quantify the global, regional, and national burden of ischemic heart disease and ischemic stroke attributable to elevated LDL‑C from 1990 to 2023.

Key results. Estimated 3.6 million LDL‑C–attributable deaths and 90.7 million DALYs in 2023, with shifting burden toward middle‑sociodemographic countries (notably India and China) despite declines in age‑standardized rates.

Clinical relevance. Highlights the large and shifting population-level impact of elevated LDL‑C, underscoring the need to scale lipid-lowering strategies as part of secondary and primary prevention globally.

Why this paper matters. Provides epidemiologic rationale to prioritize LDL‑C control and resource allocation for prevention strategies that will affect secondary prevention programs and policy.
