Great debate: preventive percutaneous coronary intervention added to optimal medical treatment should be the default treatment for non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 05 · Review (Great Debate)

Park DW, Boden WE, De Caterina R, Gallinoro E, Ahn JM, Bairey Merz CN, Kaski JC, Kang DY, et al.

Objective. To evaluate whether preventive PCI of non-flow-limiting ‘vulnerable’ plaques should be the default strategy compared with optimal medical therapy.

Key results. Summarizes evidence including the PREVENT trial reporting reduced composite events with preventive PCI, but benefits were driven largely by softer endpoints in an open‑label design and there were no significant reductions in mortality or hard outcomes; highlights imaging methods to identify high‑risk plaques and ongoing concerns about procedural risk and cost‑effectiveness.

Clinical relevance. Directly relevant to strategies aimed at preventing future acute coronary events by lesion‑targeted intervention and informs discussions on integrating preventive PCI into secondary prevention pathways.

Why this paper matters. Represents a potential paradigm shift in secondary prevention if validated, but current data are insufficient to recommend routine preventive PCI over intensive medical therapy; further robust trials are needed.
