Restricted Loss of Life Expectancy in Patients With Acute Myocardial Infarction: A Nationwide KAMIR Registry Study.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-29 · Nationwide AMI registry survival analysis

Choi JH, Choi KH, Lee JM, Park TK, Yang JH, Song YB, Hahn JY, Kim W, et al.

Objective. Quantify 3-year restricted loss of life expectancy (LOLE) after acute myocardial infarction compared with age-, sex-, and year-matched population life tables and identify high-risk subgroups.

Key results. Overall 3-year restricted LOLE was 23 days, higher in older patients, women, and those with LVEF ≤40%; LOLE declined over time from 36 to 16 days across the study period.

Clinical relevance. Provides an intuitive, patient-centered measure of residual mortality burden after AMI and highlights subgroups with substantially greater remaining loss of life expectancy.

Why this paper matters. Helps contextualize residual risk after AMI for clinicians and patients, supporting targeted secondary prevention and risk communication, and documents temporal improvement in outcomes.

Great debate: preventive percutaneous coronary intervention added to optimal medical treatment should be the default treatment for non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 05 · Opinion/debate/review article

Park DW, Boden WE, De Caterina R, Gallinoro E, Ahn JM, Bairey Merz CN, Kaski JC, Kang DY, et al.

Objective. Debate whether preventive PCI for non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques should become routine versus continued reliance on optimal medical therapy.

Key results. Argues that intracoronary imaging can identify vulnerable plaques and cites PREVENT trial signals of reduced composite events with sealing plaques, but notes benefits were driven by softer endpoints, open-label design, and lack of mortality benefit; procedural risks and cost-effectiveness remain concerns.

Clinical relevance. Frames an active clinical controversy about intervening on imaging-defined vulnerable plaques to prevent future ACS and the limitations of current evidence.

Why this paper matters. Informs clinicians about the evolving debate and evidence gaps; underscores need for definitive randomized data before adopting preventive PCI broadly for plaque stabilization.

Comparison of Health Status Among Patients With Type 1 Myocardial Infarction and Type 2 Myocardial Infarction: The CHAMP-MI Study.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-24 · Prospective comparative cohort study

Murphy SP, Tong N, Huffman JC, Luterstein E, Raghavan A, Paquette C, Lin C, Liu Y, et al.

Objective. Compare patient-reported health status trajectories after hospitalization between type 1 and type 2 myocardial infarction.

Key results. Baseline health status was similar, but patients with type 2 MI showed smaller improvements at 1 and 4 months, greater frailty, and worse physical function over follow-up.

Clinical relevance. Highlights that type 2 MI patients recover less well and have greater functional impairment, indicating different post-MI needs.

Why this paper matters. Suggests secondary prevention and rehabilitation programs should be tailored to address frailty, physical function, and broader health domains in type 2 MI, not just ischemia-focused care.

Associations of Plasma Metal Exposure With the Risk and Severity of Acute Coronary Syndrome: Evidence From Epidemiological and Adverse Outcome Pathway Analyses.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-24 · Case-control study with mediation and mixture analyses

Fu Z, Hua Z, Huang Z, Song J, Ren L, Yang L, Guo H, Liu Y, et al.

Objective. Evaluate associations between plasma metal concentrations and risk and severity of acute coronary syndrome and explore biological pathways.

Key results. Higher zinc and selenium were inversely associated with ACS risk, while lead and arsenic showed positive associations; selenium and lead associations were partially mediated by immune cell counts and proteomic analyses implicated inflammation and oxidative stress pathways.

Clinical relevance. Associations suggest certain metal exposures correlate with ACS occurrence and severity and point to potential mechanistic pathways, but causality is unproven.

Why this paper matters. May motivate further epidemiologic and mechanistic research and, if confirmed, public health measures or targeted prevention for implicated exposures, but immediate clinical impact is limited.

Pathology of Genetic Variants in Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection.

Circulation · 2026-09-01 · Autopsy pathology series with whole-exome sequencing

Tanaka T, Kawakami R, Gaynor BJ, Williams D, Augenstreich J, Sakamoto A, Jinnouchi H, Kawai K, et al.

Objective. Investigate histopathological features of SCAD lesions and the presence/impact of pathogenic genetic variants in candidate SCAD genes.

Key results. Culprit SCAD lesions had reduced medial collagen and altered smooth muscle cell phenotype; pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants were identified in ~25% (COL3A1, FBN1, FLNA) and correlated with reduced protein expression and more extensive dissection.

Clinical relevance. Provides pathological and genetic evidence linking extracellular matrix gene variants to arterial medial compromise and more severe SCAD, informing diagnostic and potentially familial evaluation.

Why this paper matters. Enhances understanding of SCAD pathogenesis and supports genetic testing and tailored follow-up in selected SCAD patients, with implications for preventing recurrent ACS from SCAD.

Door-to-Cardiac Rehabilitation Time and Clinical Outcomes in Patients Hospitalized for Acute Heart Failure: A Nationwide Japanese Registry.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-24 · Nationwide registry observational study

Katano S, Yano T, Kono Y, Sawamura A, Kanaoka K, Motokawa T, Miyamoto Y, Ohya Y, et al.

Objective. Assess how timing from hospital arrival to initiation of cardiac rehabilitation (door-to-CR time) relates to in-hospital mortality and functional outcomes in acute heart failure admissions.

Key results. CR was associated with lower in-hospital mortality across all timing quartiles versus no CR; earlier initiation (2–3 days) showed the greatest gains in functional recovery (Barthel Index), while mortality nadir occurred later (~6.1 days).

Clinical relevance. Large registry data suggest cardiac rehabilitation during acute hospitalization for heart failure reduces in-hospital mortality and improves function, with earlier initiation favoring functional recovery.

Why this paper matters. Supports early in-hospital CR delivery as a potentially modifiable care process with meaningful functional and survival associations; warrants prospective trials to define optimal timing and causality.

Real-World Effectiveness of Evolocumab in Reducing Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events: A Cohort Study.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-18 · Real-world cohort study (claims/database)

Jin R, Hurwitz K, Dhalwani N, Kent ST, Jackman L, Accortt N, Brookhart MA, Head L, et al.

Objective. Evaluate effectiveness of evolocumab in reducing major adverse cardiovascular events in routine clinical practice among patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

Key results. After adjustment, continued evolocumab use was associated with a 20% lower 4-year risk of MI/stroke/revascularization and a 29% lower risk of MI/stroke/cardiovascular death compared with early discontinuers.

Clinical relevance. Provides large-scale real-world effectiveness evidence supporting PCSK9 inhibitor benefit on hard outcomes in secondary prevention populations outside trials.

Why this paper matters. Reinforces guideline recommendations on aggressive LDL-lowering with PCSK9 inhibition for high-risk secondary prevention patients and may inform payer and practice decisions.

Septal Nonischemic Late Gadolinium Enhancement After Myocardial Infarction Is Associated With Adverse Remodeling and Improves Risk Stratification for Heart Failure Outcomes.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-24 · Cardiac MRI cohort prognostic study

Zhou P, Zhao K, Aisikaier K, Dong Z, Ma X, Tang Y, Wei Z, Jia X, et al.

Objective. Characterize prevalence and prognostic value of nonischemic late gadolinium enhancement (NI-LGE) in patients with prior myocardial infarction.

Key results. NI-LGE was present in 8.2% (predominantly septal) and was strongly associated with heart-failure–related events (sHR≈5.98) independent of LVEF and ischemic LGE extent, improving predictive discrimination for HF outcomes.

Clinical relevance. Identifies septal NI-LGE on CMR as an imaging marker of adverse remodeling and substantially higher HF-related risk after MI, potentially refining post-MI risk stratification.

Why this paper matters. May influence surveillance and tailored heart-failure prevention strategies post-MI if validated prospectively and could guide more intensive therapy or monitoring in affected patients.

Long-Term Outcomes of Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists in Patients With Peripheral Artery Disease and Type 2 Diabetes.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-07-01 · Retrospective propensity-matched cohort (electronic health records)

Rosenzveig A, Tefera L, Agrawal A, Rajendran J, Massad F, Badwan O, Wajsberg J, Chaudhury P, et al.

Objective. Examine long-term outcomes of GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy versus comparator in patients with peripheral artery disease and type 2 diabetes.

Key results. At 5 years GLP-1 RA use was associated with lower mortality, hospitalization, revascularization, and both major and minor amputations versus matched comparators; MACE and major adverse kidney events were similar.

Clinical relevance. Suggests GLP-1 RAs may confer limb- and survival-related benefits in PAD patients with diabetes, relevant to secondary prevention and therapy selection.

Why this paper matters. Supports prioritizing GLP-1 RAs in high-risk PAD patients with diabetes for potential limb-sparing and survival benefits, though residual confounding in observational data requires confirmation.

Lipidic Plaque Features of Polyvascular Disease in Response to Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol <55 mg/dL: Implication of Circulating Lipoprotein(a) Levels.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-30 · Multicenter imaging registry (near-infrared spectroscopy)

Katasako-Yabumoto A, Kataoka Y, Nicholls SJ, Kiyoshige E, Nishimura K, Puri R, Murai K, Iwai T, et al.

Objective. Assess whether achieving LDL-C <55 mg/dL associates with lower lipid-rich coronary plaque burden in patients with and without polyvascular disease, and explore role of Lp(a).

Key results. Among patients without polyvascular disease, LDL-C <55 mg/dL linked to lower lipid-core burden index and fewer high Lp(a) levels; in polyvascular disease, LDL-C <55 mg/dL did not reduce lipid-core burden and ~20% had Lp(a) ≥50 mg/dL.

Clinical relevance. Suggests coronary atheroma in polyvascular disease may be less responsive to LDL-C lowering alone and that elevated Lp(a) may identify patients needing additional lipid-targeted therapy.

Why this paper matters. Highlights a subgroup (polyvascular disease) where standard LDL lowering may be insufficient and Lp(a) assessment/targeting could be important in secondary prevention strategies.
