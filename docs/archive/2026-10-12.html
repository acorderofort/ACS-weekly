Comparison of Health Status Among Patients With Type 1 Myocardial Infarction and Type 2 Myocardial Infarction: The CHAMP-MI Study.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-24 · Prospective comparative cohort

Murphy SP, Tong N, Huffman JC, Luterstein E, Raghavan A, Paquette C, Lin C, Liu Y, et al.

Objective. Compare health status trajectories after hospitalization between patients with type 1 and type 2 myocardial infarction over 4 months.

Key results. Baseline health status similar; at 1 and 4 months patients with type 2 MI had smaller improvements, worse physical function and greater frailty than type 1 MI despite similar angina scores over time.

Clinical relevance. Highlights that patients with type 2 MI experience poorer recovery and persistent functional deficits, suggesting they may require tailored secondary prevention and rehabilitation strategies beyond ischemia-focused care.

Why this paper matters. Draws attention to an underrecognized high-need subgroup after MI (type 2) where conventional post-MI pathways may not address frailty and functional recovery.

Associations of Plasma Metal Exposure With the Risk and Severity of Acute Coronary Syndrome: Evidence From Epidemiological and Adverse Outcome Pathway Analyses.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-24 · Case-control epidemiological study

Fu Z, Hua Z, Huang Z, Song J, Ren L, Yang L, Guo H, Liu Y, et al.

Objective. Examine associations between plasma metal levels and risk/severity of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and explore mediating pathways.

Key results. Higher zinc and selenium inversely associated with ACS risk, while lead (and possibly arsenic) positively associated; selenium inversely associated with angiographic severity metrics; mediation analyses implicated immune cell counts and inflammatory/oxidative mechanisms.

Clinical relevance. Identifies potentially modifiable environmental/nutritional exposures linked to ACS risk and severity, which could inform preventive strategies and mechanistic research in ACS prevention.

Why this paper matters. Suggests plasma metal profiling may add etiologic insight into ACS risk and identifies candidate pathways for intervention, but causal inference is limited by observational design.

Multivariate Machine Learning Model for Long-Term Risk Prediction of Acute Coronary Syndrome in Patients With Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction and Obstructive Sleep Apnea.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-18 · Multicenter retrospective cohort with machine learning model development and external validation

Wang Y, Xu Y, Peng J, Hao M, Huang L, Yao Z, Ruan H, Leng J, et al.

Objective. Develop and externally validate a machine-learning model to predict long-term risk of acute coronary syndrome in patients with HFpEF and obstructive sleep apnea.

Key results. An accelerated oblique random survival forest using 10 predictors achieved C-index ~0.73 on external validation and AUCs ~0.74–0.76 at 20–40 months, with web-based calculator availability.

Clinical relevance. Provides a risk tool for ACS prediction in a specific high-risk phenotype (HFpEF+OSA) that could aid individualized prevention decisions if prospectively validated.

Why this paper matters. Offers targeted risk stratification for a niche but high-risk population; applicability outside the studied centers and clinical utility require prospective evaluation.

Door-to-Cardiac Rehabilitation Time and Clinical Outcomes in Patients Hospitalized for Acute Heart Failure: A Nationwide Japanese Registry.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-24 · Nationwide registry observational study

Katano S, Yano T, Kono Y, Sawamura A, Kanaoka K, Motokawa T, Miyamoto Y, Ohya Y, et al.

Objective. Determine the association between door-to-cardiac rehabilitation (CR) time during acute heart failure hospitalization and in-hospital mortality and functional outcomes.

Key results. All CR timing quartiles were linked to lower in-hospital mortality versus no CR (adjusted ORs 0.39–0.49); earlier CR (2–3 days) associated with better functional outcomes (Barthel Index), while mortality nadir occurred at ~6 days.

Clinical relevance. Supports early initiation of inpatient CR after acute heart failure for functional recovery and suggests a broad window for mortality benefit, informing timing policies for CR deployment.

Why this paper matters. Large registry evidence indicating timing-dependent benefits can guide systems to prioritize early CR access during acute admissions; prospective validation is needed.

Septal Nonischemic Late Gadolinium Enhancement After Myocardial Infarction Is Associated With Adverse Remodeling and Improves Risk Stratification for Heart Failure Outcomes.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-24 · Observational cohort (cardiac MRI registry)

Zhou P, Zhao K, Aisikaier K, Dong Z, Ma X, Tang Y, Wei Z, Jia X, et al.

Objective. Assess prevalence, distribution, and prognostic value of nonischemic late gadolinium enhancement (NI-LGE) in patients with prior myocardial infarction.

Key results. NI-LGE was present in 8.2% (predominantly septal) and associated with adverse remodeling; NI-LGE conferred a markedly higher risk of heart-failure–related events (sHR≈6.0) and improved model discrimination beyond LVEF and ischemic LGE extent.

Clinical relevance. NI-LGE on CMR identifies a small subgroup of post-MI patients at substantially higher risk of HF events and may refine risk stratification for secondary prevention and follow-up intensity.

Why this paper matters. Provides imaging biomarker evidence to target post-MI patients for closer surveillance or tailored HF-preventive strategies beyond standard measures.

Real-World Effectiveness of Evolocumab in Reducing Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events: A Cohort Study.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-09-18 · Real-world observational cohort (claims/EHR database)

Jin R, Hurwitz K, Dhalwani N, Kent ST, Jackman L, Accortt N, Brookhart MA, Head L, et al.

Objective. Assess real-world effectiveness of evolocumab versus discontinuation on rates of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in adults with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

Key results. After adjustment, continued evolocumab use was associated with a 20% lower 4-year risk of a composite first outcome (MI, stroke, revascularization) and a 29% lower risk of MI/stroke/CV death compared with early discontinuation.

Clinical relevance. Supports effectiveness of PCSK9 inhibition with evolocumab for secondary prevention in routine practice, reinforcing guideline recommendations for high-risk patients not at LDL-C goal.

Why this paper matters. Large, contemporary real-world evidence complements randomized trial data and underscores potential population-level benefits of maintaining PCSK9 inhibitor therapy.

Lipidic Plaque Features of Polyvascular Disease in Response to Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol <55 mg/dL: Implication of Circulating Lipoprotein(a) Levels.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-30 · Multicenter registry imaging study (near-infrared spectroscopy)

Katasako-Yabumoto A, Kataoka Y, Nicholls SJ, Kiyoshige E, Nishimura K, Puri R, Murai K, Iwai T, et al.

Objective. Evaluate whether achieving LDL-C <55 mg/dL is associated with decreased lipid-rich coronary plaque burden in patients with and without polyvascular disease (PolyVD) and examine Lp(a) implications.

Key results. In patients without PolyVD, LDL-C <55 mg/dL was linked to lower lipid-core burden index and lower frequency of Lp(a) ≥50 mg/dL; in PolyVD patients, LDL-C <55 mg/dL did not associate with lower lipid burden and >20% had elevated Lp(a).

Clinical relevance. Suggests residual lipid-rich plaque burden despite low LDL-C in polyvascular disease and implicates elevated Lp(a) as a potential therapeutic target in this high-risk subgroup for secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Identifies a subset (PolyVD) less responsive to LDL-C lowering alone, supporting consideration of adjunctive therapies targeting Lp(a) or alternative strategies to further reduce plaque lipid content.

Restricted Loss of Life Expectancy in Patients With Acute Myocardial Infarction: A Nationwide KAMIR Registry Study.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-08-29 · Nationwide registry cohort study

Choi JH, Choi KH, Lee JM, Park TK, Yang JH, Song YB, Hahn JY, Kim W, et al.

Objective. Quantify restricted loss of life expectancy (LOLE) over 3 years after acute myocardial infarction and examine subgroup differences and temporal trends.

Key results. Overall 3-year restricted LOLE was 23 days, higher in older patients, women, and those with low LVEF; LOLE decreased over time from 36 to 16 days across the study period.

Clinical relevance. Provides an intuitive, patient-centered metric of residual post-MI risk to inform prognosis communication and prioritization of secondary prevention measures.

Why this paper matters. Demonstrates that while residual life-expectancy loss after MI is measurable, it is relatively modest on average and has improved over time—useful for counseling and risk stratification.

Long-Term Outcomes of Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists in Patients With Peripheral Artery Disease and Type 2 Diabetes.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-07-01 · Retrospective cohort (TriNetX) with propensity-score matching

Rosenzveig A, Tefera L, Agrawal A, Rajendran J, Massad F, Badwan O, Wajsberg J, Chaudhury P, et al.

Objective. Assess long-term cardiovascular and limb outcomes of GLP-1 receptor agonists versus metformin in patients with peripheral artery disease (PAD) and type 2 diabetes.

Key results. GLP-1 RA use was associated with lower 5-year mortality, hospitalization, revascularization, and rates of major and minor amputation versus metformin in matched cohorts; MACE and major adverse kidney events were similar.

Clinical relevance. Supports prioritizing GLP-1 RAs in high-risk PAD patients with diabetes for reduction in limb events and mortality as part of secondary prevention strategies.

Why this paper matters. Adds real-world evidence that GLP-1 RAs may confer limb-protective as well as survival benefits in PAD with diabetes, which may influence therapeutic selection pending randomized data.

Great debate: preventive percutaneous coronary intervention added to optimal medical treatment should be the default treatment for non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 05 · Narrative 'Great Debate' review/opinion piece

Park DW, Boden WE, De Caterina R, Gallinoro E, Ahn JM, Bairey Merz CN, Kaski JC, Kang DY, et al.

Objective. Debate whether preventive PCI of non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques should be the default management over optimal medical therapy.

Key results. Authors summarize imaging capability to detect vulnerable plaques and cite PREVENT trial signals for reduced composite endpoints with preventive PCI but note lack of mortality/hard endpoint benefit, potential procedural risks, and cost-effectiveness concerns.

Clinical relevance. Frames an important clinical controversy about invasive plaque-sealing versus intensified medical therapy for secondary prevention, highlighting evidence gaps and need for further trials.

Why this paper matters. Stimulates critical appraisal of changing paradigms in preventing future ACS events from non-obstructive vulnerable plaques and underscores that current data are insufficient to change standard practice.
