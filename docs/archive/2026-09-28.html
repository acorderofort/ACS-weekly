Acute Coronary Syndrome in Kawasaki Disease From Infancy to Adulthood.

Circulation · 2026-09-21 · Narrative review

Jone PN, Newburger JW, Burns JC, Tomey MI, Jakob A, Shiono Y, Fukushima S, Mitani Y, et al.

Objective. To synthesize evidence on epidemiology, pathophysiology, imaging, acute management, revascularization, antithrombotic therapy, and follow-up for ACS arising from Kawasaki disease (KD) across the lifespan.

Key results. KD-associated ACS differs fundamentally from atherosclerotic ACS in mechanisms (aneurysms, thrombosis), anatomy, and management implications; multidisciplinary, disease-specific approaches and modern imaging/interventional strategies are recommended.

Clinical relevance. Provides practical, disease-specific guidance for recognizing and managing ACS in KD patients and stresses collaboration between pediatric and adult cardiology teams.

Why this paper matters. Addresses an underserved clinical scenario where standard atherosclerotic ACS protocols may be inappropriate, improving care for growing numbers of adults with childhood KD coronary sequelae.

CKD disrupts cardiac energy metabolism and aggravates cardiac inflammation, oxidative stress, and dysfunction post-infarction.

Cardiovascular research · 2026 Sep 24 · Translational study (mouse adenine-induced CKD model with human tissue/blood validation)

Schulte C, Wollenhaupt J, Hourtz S, Harlacher E, Moellmann J, Cruz Junho CV, Kohlhaas M, Jankowski V, et al.

Objective. To investigate how chronic kidney disease (CKD) affects cardiac remodeling, inflammation, metabolism, and function after myocardial infarction.

Key results. CKD worsened post-MI cardiac dysfunction without changing infarct size, associated with increased myeloid cell infiltration, elevated S100A8/A9 and p38 signaling, oxidative stress, and altered cardiac metabolism (reduced glycolysis and CoA availability); some findings corroborated in human infarcted hearts and CKD patients.

Clinical relevance. Identifies immune, inflammatory, and metabolic pathways by which CKD may worsen post-MI outcomes, pointing to potential translational targets for high-risk post-MI patients with renal dysfunction.

Why this paper matters. Explains mechanisms underlying poorer post-MI prognosis in CKD and suggests directions for research into tailored therapies to improve outcomes after ACS in patients with kidney disease.

Sex influence on in-hospital and long-term prognosis of myocardial infarction with non-obstructive coronary artery disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 22 · Systematic review and meta-analysis

Herrera-Flores C, Vernooij RWM, Herrera-Flores J, Martínez-Sellés M

Objective. To evaluate sex differences in in-hospital and long-term prognosis of patients with myocardial infarction with non-obstructive coronary arteries (MINOCA).

Key results. Pooled data from 15 studies (n≈42,870) showed no sex differences in in-hospital or long-term all-cause mortality for MINOCA overall; women had higher stroke risk during follow-up, and women with MINOCA had lower in-hospital mortality than women with MI due to obstructive CAD.

Clinical relevance. Consolidates evidence that MINOCA prognosis is largely similar between sexes, with some sex-specific differences in stroke risk and in-hospital comparisons to MI-CAD, informing risk communication and follow-up strategies.

Why this paper matters. Supports the need for tailored evaluation and follow-up in MINOCA while indicating that sex alone may not necessitate different prognostic expectations for mortality.

Sex-specific V̇O2peak reference values and their association with major adverse cardiovascular events in Norwegian cardiac rehabilitation patients.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 22 · Retrospective cohort (CPET data from inpatient cardiac rehabilitation)

Svenningsen A, Hollekim-Strand SM, Sandmæl JA, Engan H, Wisløff U, Letnes JM

Objective. To generate sex- and diagnosis-specific V̇O2peak reference values in Norwegian CAD patients in cardiac rehabilitation and assess associations between V̇O2peak and major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE).

Key results. In 1,651 CAD patients, each 1 mL·kg-1·min-1 higher V̇O2peak was associated with 7% lower MACE risk in men and 5% lower risk in women; highest fitness quartile had ~55% lower risk versus lowest quartile.

Clinical relevance. Provides usable V̇O2peak reference values for rehab populations and reinforces cardiorespiratory fitness as a strong, independent prognostic marker to guide risk stratification and individualized rehab targets.

Why this paper matters. Supports routine CPET-based fitness assessment during cardiac rehabilitation and using V̇O2peak to tailor intensity and prognostic counseling.

Association of Adherence to Various Dietary Patterns With Mortality Among Survivors of Myocardial Infarction: A Prospective Cohort Study.

Circulation · 2026-08-25 · Prospective cohort study (Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-Up Study)

Ma L, Hu Y, Liu G, Rexrode KM, Manson JE, Hu FB, Rimm EB, Sun Q

Objective. To evaluate adherence to multiple healthy dietary patterns after nonfatal MI and the association of post-MI diet quality (and changes in adherence) with long-term mortality and recurrent events.

Key results. Among 5,895 MI survivors, higher post-MI adherence to eight healthy dietary patterns was consistently associated with lower total mortality and lower cardiovascular mortality and recurrent nonfatal MI (e.g., highest vs lowest quintile AHEI HR 0.67). Improvement in diet quality after MI was also linked to reduced mortality.

Clinical relevance. Strong observational evidence supports promoting overall high-quality diets (e.g., Mediterranean/AHEI/DASH patterns) as part of secondary prevention after MI.

Why this paper matters. Diet quality after MI is a modifiable factor associated with substantially improved long-term survival and fewer recurrent events; findings are directly applicable to secondary prevention counseling.

Heart Rate and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Post-Myocardial Infarction Patients Treated by β-Blockers: A Secondary Analysis of the ABYSS Trial.

Circulation · 2026-06-10 · Prespecified secondary analysis of a randomized controlled trial (ABYSS)

Zeitouni M, Procopi N, Cayla G, Ferrari E, Rangé G, Puymirat E, Delarche N, Guedeney P, et al.

Objective. To examine associations between prerandomization heart rate, β-blocker continuation vs interruption, and cardiovascular outcomes in stable post-MI patients with LVEF ≥40%.

Key results. Higher baseline HR was linked to increased risk of death, MI, or stroke and higher mortality; interruption of β-blockers raised HR by ~10–13 bpm and was consistently associated with worse outcomes across HR strata.

Clinical relevance. Supports continued β-blocker therapy after uncomplicated MI in patients with preserved LVEF and highlights on-treatment HR as a relevant prognostic marker.

Why this paper matters. Provides randomized-trial–based support that stopping β-blockers post-MI increases heart rate and is linked to worse outcomes, informing medication management in secondary prevention.

Sex differences in medication adherence and medication-associated mortality after coronary artery bypass grafting.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 22 · Observational comparative study using nationwide drug purchase registers (post-CABG cohort)

Nurkkala J, Winstén A, Toivonen J, Niiranen T, FinnGen

Objective. To assess sex differences in long-term adherence to secondary prevention medications after CABG and associations with mortality.

Key results. In 9,857 CABG patients, adherence to statins, beta-blockers, and RAAS inhibitors remained high over 10 years with no major sex differences; beta-blocker adherence was associated with a greater mortality benefit in women than men.

Clinical relevance. Reassures that long-term adherence after CABG is generally high and suggests potential sex-differential benefit of beta-blockers that may warrant further study.

Why this paper matters. Findings inform secondary prevention strategies after CABG and highlight the need to consider sex when evaluating medication effects and designing interventional trials.

Long-term prognosis of unrecognized myocardial infarction in women and men from the general population: the Rotterdam Study.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 22 · Prospective population-based cohort (Rotterdam Study)

van Oortmerssen JAE, Zuo L, Tilly MJ, Hummel B, Ikram MK, Boersma E, Roeters van Lennep JE, Kavousi M

Objective. To determine sex-specific long-term prognosis of unrecognized myocardial infarction (UMI) compared with recognized MI and no MI in the general population.

Key results. UMI was associated with increased risks of heart failure and all-cause mortality, with differing patterns by sex (stronger, statistically significant associations in men across several outcomes); adjustment for risk factors attenuated some associations in women.

Clinical relevance. Highlights that UMI confers prognostic risk and that sex modifies long-term outcomes, underscoring the importance of detection strategies and tailored secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Supports consideration of UMI identification (e.g., ECG screening, imaging) as a step to target secondary prevention measures, especially in men where risk appeared higher.

Sex-specific disparities in lipid-based prediction of major cardiovascular events.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Sep 22 · Prospective cohort comparative study of lipid markers in high-risk patients

Schnetzer L, Muendlein A, Festa A, Saely CH, Drexel H, Leiherer A

Objective. To evaluate sex differences in levels and predictive performance of several lipid-based markers (LDL-C, remnant cholesterol, ApoA-1, TG/HDL, ceramide scores) for major cardiovascular events.

Key results. Remnant cholesterol predicted MACE in both sexes; ApoA-1 and TG/HDL predicted events in women only, while ceramide scores predicted events in men only; LDL-C had limited predictive value in this high-risk cohort.

Clinical relevance. Suggests that sex-specific adaptation of lipid biomarker use could improve risk stratification in secondary prevention populations.

Why this paper matters. May inform more individualized lipid-related risk assessment and biomarker selection in secondary prevention, but requires validation before routine practice change.
