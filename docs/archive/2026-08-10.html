Emergency Medical Service Identification and Prehospital Care of Acute Coronary Syndrome Among Culturally and Linguistically Diverse Immigrants: Statewide Linkage Study.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-07-31 · Retrospective cohort (ambulance-hospital linkage)

Olani AB, Howell S, Eastwood K, Nehme Z, Mahony E, Nehme E, Stub D, Bray JE

Objective. To assess whether EMS identification, prehospital care, and times for patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) differ by culturally and linguistically diverse (CALD) immigrant status.

Key results. Among 28,557 ACS cases, 30.2% were CALD; paramedic ACS identification was lower in CALD patients preferring a language other than English (CALD-LOTE 57.2% vs Australian-born 69.0%; adjusted OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.76–0.92). Time-critical dispatch rates were similar, and CALD patients were more often taken directly to PCI-capable hospitals; small rural EMS time differences were noted.

Clinical relevance. Identifies a disparity in prehospital ACS recognition for non–English-speaking immigrants that could contribute to delayed diagnosis and treatment despite higher direct transfers to PCI-capable centers.

Why this paper matters. Highlights the need for tailored EMS training, language-access strategies, and system interventions to reduce prehospital diagnostic disparity and potentially improve ACS outcomes in CALD populations.

Korean Artificial Intelligence-Based Risk Model for Predicting Mortality After Acute Myocardial Infarction.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-07-25 · Model development and external validation (machine learning using registry data)

Kim SH, Yoo JH, Park JJ, Yoon CH, Tobin S, Campagnari C, Yagil A, Chae IH, et al.

Objective. To develop and externally validate simplified machine-learning models (KARMA) predicting 3-month and 3-year mortality after acute myocardial infarction using routinely available clinical variables.

Key results. KARMA models achieved high discrimination: 3-month AUC 0.91 (95% CI 0.88–0.94) and 3-year AUC 0.85 (95% CI 0.82–0.87), outperforming GRACE and KAMIR scores; external validation showed consistent performance (AUC 0.92 for 3-month; 0.86 for 3-year).

Clinical relevance. Offers a contemporary, parsimonious risk tool to identify high-risk post-AMI patients who may benefit from intensified secondary prevention, surveillance, or resource allocation.

Why this paper matters. Improved post-AMI risk stratification using validated, modern models can guide personalized secondary prevention and follow-up strategies; implementation and impact on management remain to be tested.

Outcomes after a first acute myocardial infarction in patients with or without congenital heart disease.

European heart journal · 2026 Aug 03 · Nationwide matched cohort study

Holmgren L, von Koch S, Andell P, Sörensson P, Christersson C, Trzebiatowska-Krzynska A, Fedchenko M, Mandelanakis Z, et al.

Objective. To compare all-cause mortality and recurrent acute myocardial infarction after a first AMI in adults with congenital heart disease (ACHD) versus those without ACHD.

Key results. Before matching ACHD patients were younger; in matched analysis there was no significant difference in 1-year mortality or recurrent AMI; a higher unadjusted 10-year mortality (HR 1.4, 95% CI 1.0–1.9) attenuated after adjustment.

Clinical relevance. Indicates that short-term outcomes after first AMI in ACHD patients are similar to matched controls, supporting application of standard secondary prevention strategies, with attention to long-term follow-up.

Why this paper matters. Reassures clinicians that conventional post-AMI secondary prevention principles are applicable to many ACHD patients but underscores need for individualized long-term care and monitoring.

Women's heart centres: a clinical consensus statement of the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging (EACVI), the European Association of Percutaneous Cardiovascular Interventions (EAPCI), the Heart Failure Association (HFA), and the Association for Acute CardioVascular Care (ACVC) of the ESC.

European heart journal · 2026 Aug 03 · Clinical consensus statement

Grapsa J, Almeida AG, Sambola A, Vitale C, Appelman Y, Shivalkar B, Anderson LJ, Trabattoni D, et al.

Objective. To define the rationale, structure, and operational framework for Women's Heart Centres (WHCs) to deliver multidisciplinary, sex-sensitive cardiovascular care across the life course, including cardiac rehabilitation.

Key results. Proposes hub-and-spoke WHC models, referral pathways, training competencies, and domains (eg, ischemia with nonobstructive coronaries, cardio-obstetrics, autoimmune disease, mental health, and cardiac rehabilitation); notes limited long-term outcome data but suggests improved diagnostic precision and risk-factor control in available evidence.

Clinical relevance. Provides a practical blueprint for integrating sex-specific assessment and delivery of cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention services to address gaps in care for women.

Why this paper matters. Could reduce sex-based disparities in access to and quality of cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention if adopted, but requires evaluation of implementation and outcome impact.

A scoping review of recommendations for adults with cardiovascular disease to remain physically active during hot weather.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Aug 03 · Scoping review

Mornas A, Deshayes TA, Bartlett AA, Chaseling G, Jay O, Marzolini S, Simard F, Iglesies-Grau J, et al.

Objective. To map existing temperature thresholds and behavioral recommendations for adults with cardiovascular disease to remain physically active during hot weather.

Key results. Of 32 sources, 24 reported at least one temperature above which activity should be limited; most addressed preventive measures and symptoms, but none provided CVD-specific actionable recommendations and no sources explicitly addressed adverse cardiac events as risks.

Clinical relevance. Identifies a lack of tailored guidance for cardiac rehabilitation programs and patients with cardiovascular disease regarding safe exercise during hot weather, a growing concern with climate change.

Why this paper matters. Reveals a practical gap that limits clinicians' ability to counsel and adapt rehabilitation/exercise prescriptions for patients during heat exposure; calls for development of CVD-specific, evidence-based recommendations.

Impact of Drug-Coated Balloon Inflation Time on Outcome After Percutaneous Coronary Intervention: An Observational Study From SCAAR.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-07-31 · Observational registry analysis (SCAAR)

von Koch S, Zhou M, Håkansson A, Råmunddal T, Omerovic E, Zwackman S, Erlinge D, Rissanen TT, et al.

Objective. To evaluate whether drug-coated balloon (DCB) inflation time ≥30 seconds versus <30 seconds is associated with better 1-year outcomes after percutaneous coronary intervention.

Key results. In 11,238 PCI patients treated with DCBs, inflation ≥30s (n=10,826) vs <30s (n=412) was associated with lower 1-year target segment revascularization (3.2% vs 6.4%; adjusted HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.32–0.69) and lower target vessel myocardial infarction (1.8% vs 5.3%; adjusted HR 0.32, 95% CI 0.21–0.50); no difference in all-cause mortality was observed.

Clinical relevance. Suggests that longer DCB inflation times may reduce restenosis-related events and target-vessel MI after PCI, a potentially modifiable intra-procedural factor relevant to secondary prevention of ischemic events.

Why this paper matters. Provides real-world registry evidence that could inform procedural technique and quality improvement to lower repeat revascularization in patients treated with DCBs, but randomized confirmation is needed due to observational design.
