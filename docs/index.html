Cangrelor versus crushed ticagrelor in acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock (DAPT-SHOCK-AMI): a randomised, double-blind trial.

Lancet (London, England) · 2026-11-07 · Randomized, double-blind, multicenter trial (DAPT-SHOCK-AMI)

Motovska Z, Hlinomaz O, Mrozek J, Akin I, Kala P, Montalescot G, Hromadka M, Geisler T, et al.

Objective. Compare intravenous cangrelor versus crushed oral ticagrelor for platelet inhibition and 30-day clinical outcomes in patients with AMI complicated by cardiogenic shock.

Key results. Cangrelor achieved effective platelet inhibition at the end of PCI far more often than crushed ticagrelor (100% vs 22% in analysed patients). Non-inferiority for the 30-day composite clinical endpoint (death, MI, stroke) was not demonstrated (38% vs 41%; difference -4 percentage points; Pnon-inferiority=0.13).

Clinical relevance. Provides randomized data on P2Y12 strategies in the high-risk AMI–cardiogenic shock population, showing superior immediate platelet inhibition with cangrelor but no clear short-term clinical advantage.

Why this paper matters. Guides antiplatelet choice in AMI with shock where oral absorption may be unreliable; highlights need for further trials powered for clinical outcomes.

Multimodality Cardiac Imaging Substudy of the DAPT-SHOCK-AMI Randomized Trial: Echocardiographic and Cardiac Magnetic Resonance Assessment of Cangrelor versus Crushed Ticagrelor in Cardiogenic Shock Complicating Acute Myocardial Infarction.

Circulation · 2026-11-07 · Prespecified multimodality imaging substudy of a randomized trial (DAPT-SHOCK-AMI)

Motovska Z, Ionita O, Guglielmo M, Hromadka M, Baxa J, Tarantini R, Kotrc M, Panovsky R, et al.

Objective. Assess whether cangrelor versus crushed ticagrelor affects microvascular injury, tissue characterization, and ventricular recovery after AMI complicated by cardiogenic shock using serial echo and CMR.

Key results. Cangrelor was associated with less acute microvascular obstruction on CMR, lower remote myocardium native T1 at 1 year, and greater recovery of LV function over 1 year versus crushed ticagrelor, without difference in infarct size.

Clinical relevance. Suggests potential microcirculatory and remote myocardial benefits of cangrelor despite neutral short-term clinical endpoint, hypothesis-generating for mechanism and longer-term outcomes.

Why this paper matters. Imaging signals may explain clinical equipoise and inform mechanistic hypotheses and future endpoint-powered studies in AMI–shock.

Variability of lipoprotein(a) levels in patients with acute myocardial infarction between admission, discharge, and follow-up.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026-11-08 · Prospective multicentre cohort (FRENCHIE-Lp(a))

Simon T, Khani Bittar R, Rousseau A, Prunier F, Cayla G, Ferrari E, Lhermusier T, Berard L, et al.

Objective. Describe temporal variability of lipoprotein(a) (Lp[a]) measured at admission, discharge, and 3–12 months after acute myocardial infarction and explore pragmatic testing strategies.

Key results. Median Lp(a) was stable overall, but about one-third of patients changed Lp(a) category; a discharge threshold of ≤37 mg/dL had <1% misclassification for follow-up Lp(a) >70 mg/dL, suggesting a two-step testing strategy.

Clinical relevance. Provides practical guidance on timing and interpretation of Lp(a) testing after AMI to identify patients for Lp(a)-targeted therapies/trials.

Why this paper matters. Helps clinicians decide when to measure Lp(a) around an AMI and reduces misclassification when selecting patients for emerging Lp(a) interventions.

High Bleeding Risk and Aspirin Omission at the Time of Primary PCI in STEMI: A Post Hoc Analysis of the PREMIUM Trial.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-10-31 · Post hoc analysis of a randomized trial (PREMIUM)

Inaba S, Ogita M, Suwa S, Kashiwabara K, Wada H, Endo H, Shitara J, Nanasato M, et al.

Objective. Determine whether high bleeding risk (ARC-HBR) modifies the effect of upfront aspirin omission (prasugrel monotherapy vs DAPT) after primary PCI for STEMI.

Key results. HBR patients had substantially higher ischemic and bleeding risks, but HBR status did not identify a subgroup with clearly favorable effect from upfront aspirin omission; interaction tests were non-significant.

Clinical relevance. Does not support using HBR alone to guide upfront aspirin omission at primary PCI for STEMI in the context of the low-dose prasugrel regimen studied.

Why this paper matters. Addresses personalized antiplatelet de-escalation in STEMI; suggests caution before using HBR status alone to omit aspirin immediately post-PCI.

Long-Term Survival After Initial Invasive or Conservative Management of Stable Coronary Disease in ISCHEMIA-EXTEND.

JAMA · 2026-11-07 · Long-term follow-up of a randomized trial (ISCHEMIA-EXTEND)

Hochman JS, Anthopolos R, Reynolds HR, Bangalore S, Xu Y, O'Brien SM, Boumakis S, Chang M, et al.

Objective. Compare long-term all-cause and cause-specific mortality after an initial invasive versus conservative strategy in patients with chronic coronary disease.

Key results. Over median 9 years, 11-year all-cause mortality did not differ significantly between invasive and conservative strategies (25.8% vs 26.9%; adjusted HR 0.97). Posterior probability of >1% survival benefit with invasive strategy was low (18%).

Clinical relevance. Important long-term randomized evidence that routine invasive management does not confer a meaningful overall survival benefit over conservative management in stable ischemic heart disease.

Why this paper matters. Influences long-term management decisions for stable coronary disease and supports selective invasive management rather than routine invasive strategies.

Long-Term Survival After Initial Invasive or Conservative Management of Stable Coronary Disease in Patients With Advanced Chronic Kidney Disease: ISCHEMIA-CKD EXTEND.

Circulation · 2026-11-07 · Extended follow-up of randomized trial (ISCHEMIA-CKD EXTEND)

Bangalore S, Maron DJ, Anthopolos R, Reynolds HR, Xu Y, Chang M, Boumakis S, Lopez-Sendon J, et al.

Objective. Assess long-term mortality after initial invasive versus conservative management in patients with moderate/severe ischemia and advanced chronic kidney disease.

Key results. Over median 8 years, no significant difference in all-cause or cardiovascular mortality between invasive and conservative strategies; highest risks were observed overall, especially in hemodialysis patients.

Clinical relevance. Provides long-term randomized data in an understudied high-risk CKD population showing no clear survival benefit from routine invasive strategy.

Why this paper matters. Helps tailor revascularization decisions in patients with advanced CKD where procedural risk is high and long-term benefit uncertain.

Prognostic Implication of CYP2C19 Genotype During Chronic Clopidogrel Monotherapy After Percutaneous Coronary Intervention.

JACC. Asia · 2026 Nov · Post hoc pooled analysis / observational analysis of trial and registry data

Jo J, Lee SY, Choi KH, Song YB, Kim BK, Joo HJ, Chang K, Hwang JY, et al.

Objective. Evaluate whether CYP2C19 loss-of-function genotype affects outcomes during chronic clopidogrel monotherapy after PCI.

Key results. In 1,352 patients, outcomes at 3 years (composite of death, MI, stroke) and major bleeding did not differ significantly between intermediate/poor metabolizers and normal/rapid metabolizers.

Clinical relevance. Provides reassurance that clopidogrel monotherapy for long-term maintenance after PCI had consistent outcomes across CYP2C19 genotypes in the studied cohorts.

Why this paper matters. Addresses genotype-guided antiplatelet concerns for long-term clopidogrel monotherapy after completion of DAPT.

AI-Identified Reasons for Nonescalation of Lipid-Lowering Therapy for Secondary Prevention.

JAMA cardiology · 2026-11-07 · Retrospective cohort with natural-language model extraction (large health system)

Somani S, Kim DD, Ramaraju V, Ngo S, Babu C, Bouladian S, Chen T, King S, et al.

Objective. Use large language models to identify treat-to-target LDL strategies and documented reasons for nonescalation of lipid-lowering therapy in patients with established ASCVD and above-goal LDL-C.

Key results. Among 13,394 patients with above-goal LDL-C, a treat-to-target strategy was documented in 28%; LLT was not escalated in 88.7% of encounters, with common reasons being adverse effects, LDL-C near goal, patient preference, and deferral for repeat labs.

Clinical relevance. Quantifies system-level gaps and common documented reasons for failure to intensify secondary prevention lipid therapy using scalable NLP methods.

Why this paper matters. Identifies concrete targets (adverse-effect management, shared decision processes, workflow fixes) for interventions to improve secondary prevention LDL management at scale.

Statin Intolerance, Treatment Gaps, and Cardiovascular Outcomes: A 24-Year Health System Analysis.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-11-02 · Longitudinal health-system analysis (24 years, new statin users)

Supriami K, Akl EW, Abou-Karam R, Thompson AG, Yudelman M, Atlas SJ, Ellinor PT, Fahed AC

Objective. Characterize clinician-documented statin intolerance, subsequent lipid-lowering management, and associated cardiovascular outcomes.

Key results. Among 254,035 new statin users, 4.2% had documented intolerance; 39.9% discontinued all LLT within 90 days and <10% received nonstatin alternatives; intolerance was associated with higher rates of revascularization, MI, and stroke, and complete LLT discontinuation identified a high-risk subgroup.

Clinical relevance. Highlights real-world consequences of statin intolerance and gaps in replacement strategies, with implications for secondary prevention loss when therapy is stopped.

Why this paper matters. Supports active reassessment and use of alternative LLT strategies to avoid high-risk care gaps in secondary prevention.

TECTONIC CAD: Pivotal IDE Evaluation of Coronary Intravascular Lithotripsy for Severely Calcified Lesions.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026-10-22 · Prospective, multicenter single-arm pivotal IDE study (TECTONIC CAD)

Secemsky EA, Ali ZA, Shlofmitz E, Chehab BM, Jeremias A, Nicholson W, Sandesara P, Sayfo S, et al.

Objective. Evaluate safety and effectiveness of coronary intravascular lithotripsy (SonicForce) for severely calcified de novo coronary lesions.

Key results. Met prespecified 30-day safety (target lesion failure 0.3%) and effectiveness (procedural success 97%) endpoints; OCT substudy showed calcium fracture in ~62–66% and favorable stent expansion.

Clinical relevance. Supports intravascular lithotripsy as an effective vessel-preparation tool for severely calcified lesions to achieve good stent expansion, relevant to procedural success in patients requiring revascularization.

Why this paper matters. May expand safe options for treating heavily calcified coronary disease and improve procedural outcomes that matter for secondary prevention.

Coronary CT Angiography versus Intravascular Ultrasound Guidance for PCI.

The New England journal of medicine · 2026-11-01 · Randomized, open-label, noninferiority trial with blinded adjudication

Collet C, Mizukami T, Bouisset F, Pontone G, Zivelonghi C, Jones DA, Wilgenhof A, Støttrup NB, et al.

Objective. Compare CCTA-guided PCI planning to IVUS-guided PCI with respect to 12‑month target-vessel failure.

Key results. CCTA-guided PCI was noninferior to IVUS-guided PCI for target-vessel failure at 12 months (4.1% vs 4.6%; risk difference -0.42 percentage points; P<0.001 for noninferiority).

Clinical relevance. Suggests preprocedural CCTA planning can be a viable alternative to intraprocedural IVUS guidance for PCI in selected patients.

Why this paper matters. May support wider use of noninvasive planning to inform PCI strategy while preserving clinical outcomes; relevant to procedural aspects of secondary prevention.
