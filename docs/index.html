Association of beta-blocker therapy with outcomes after ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction stratified by GRACE risk score: A nationwide registry analysis.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-10-09 · Post-hoc analysis of two nationwide prospective registries (multicenter registry analysis)

Choi BG, Yu H, Rha SW, Ahn J, Lee SU, Kim YH, Jeong MH, Oh DJ, et al.

Objective. To determine whether associations between beta‑blocker use at discharge after STEMI and outcomes vary across GRACE risk strata in a contemporary PCI/DES-treated population.

Key results. After propensity matching, discharge beta‑blocker use was associated with lower 1‑year all‑cause mortality (2.7% vs 4.2%; HR 0.63) and MACE; benefit was absent in low‑risk (GRACE <85) patients and most pronounced in intermediate‑risk (GRACE 85–179) patients.

Clinical relevance. Supports risk‑stratified consideration of routine beta‑blocker continuation after STEMI in the era of early revascularization, suggesting limited benefit in lowest‑risk patients.

Why this paper matters. Provides real‑world evidence that the absolute benefit of discharge beta‑blockers after contemporary STEMI varies by baseline risk and should inform trial design or guideline nuance rather than immediate practice change.

Sex-specific differences in acute coronary syndrome management and outcomes: How age and treatment gaps mask a female survival advantage.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-09-20 · Nationwide registry comparative study

Sári C, Bárdos-Csenteri O, Takács P, Paksicza LM, Zsámbok R, Bakó Z, Andréka P

Objective. To examine sex‑specific differences in ACS management and all‑cause mortality using the Hungarian National Myocardial Infarction Register (2014–2024).

Key results. Females were older and received less intensive treatment; unadjusted mortality was higher in females, but after propensity matching females had a substantially lower all‑cause mortality than males (10.3 percentage‑point difference), with heterogeneity by age and ACS subtype.

Clinical relevance. Highlights sex‑based disparities in ACS care and the potential for residual confounding to mask true outcome differences; indicates priorities for equitable delivery of guideline‑recommended therapies.

Why this paper matters. Important registry evidence that disparities in treatment access and case‑mix must be addressed to improve outcomes and interpret sex differences in ACS mortality.

Lipoprotein(a) in acute myocardial infarction - insights from the randomised ASSAIL-MI trial.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-09-15 · Randomized controlled trial (biomarker subanalysis of ASSAIL‑MI)

Broch K, Andreassen AK, Anstensrud AK, Woxholt S, Flø A, Bjørkelund E, Ueland T, Halvorsen B, et al.

Objective. To assess stability of plasma Lp(a) concentrations during STEMI and whether IL‑6 receptor inhibition (tocilizumab) affects Lp(a) dynamics and associations with myocardial injury.

Key results. Lp(a) increased slightly in the days after STEMI and remained modestly elevated at 6 months; tocilizumab did not alter Lp(a) levels, and baseline Lp(a) was not associated with myocardial salvage, infarct size, or microvascular obstruction.

Clinical relevance. Indicates that acute STEMI causes small, likely clinically trivial changes in Lp(a) and that short‑term anti‑IL‑6 therapy does not modify Lp(a); supports using stable Lp(a) measures post‑MI for risk stratification.

Why this paper matters. Provides randomized trial data on biomarker behavior in ACS, informing timing of Lp(a) measurement and interpretation after MI for secondary prevention planning.

Inflammation and lipoprotein(a) as predictors of cardiovascular events across baseline risk levels - results from the UK Biobank.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-10-02 · Prospective cohort analysis (UK Biobank)

Tucker B, Ong KL, Payne T, Robledo KP, Keech A, Patel S

Objective. To assess how hsCRP and Lp(a) predict major adverse cardiovascular events across baseline cardiovascular risk strata, including secondary prevention participants.

Key results. HsCRP was consistently associated with MACE across all risk groups and strongest in secondary prevention; Lp(a) predicted MACE robustly in women across subgroups but in men primarily in primary prevention, with no Lp(a) association in male secondary prevention participants.

Clinical relevance. Supports selective measurement of hsCRP and Lp(a) to refine risk stratification, and highlights sex‑ and risk‑specific differences that may affect secondary prevention risk assessment and targeting of emerging therapies.

Why this paper matters. Large, long‑term cohort evidence relevant to biomarker‑guided risk stratification in secondary prevention and for designing sex‑aware intervention studies.

Lipoprotein(a) reduction with PCSK9-targeting pharmacotherapy: A prospective real-world registry study.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-09-23 · Prospective single‑center registry study

Kafol J, Fras Z, Novakovic M, Cevc M, Krevel B, Kafol L, Kelenc A, Kepic K, et al.

Objective. To evaluate real‑world effects of PCSK9‑targeting therapies (alirocumab, evolocumab, inclisiran) on lipoprotein(a) concentrations over time.

Key results. PCSK9‑targeted therapies produced modest, sustained Lp(a) reductions (~‑15% to ‑19% overall at later timepoints); no statistically significant differences between agents after adjustment, though inclisiran estimates were less precise.

Clinical relevance. Provides pragmatic data that currently available PCSK9‑targeted agents yield modest Lp(a) lowering in routine practice, relevant when considering Lp(a) management in patients needing secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Real‑world magnitude of Lp(a) lowering is smaller than reported in some trials, underscoring the need for dedicated Lp(a)–lowering therapies for high‑risk secondary prevention patients.
