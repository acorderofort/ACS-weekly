Interleukin-6 and C-reactive protein prognostic value following acute coronary syndrome.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 26 · Post hoc analysis of a randomized controlled trial (ODYSSEY OUTCOMES)

Jukema JW, Van Neer NJ, Szarek M, Cobbaert C, Bittner VA, Schwertfeger M, Bhatt DL, Fazio S, et al.

Objective. Determine whether hsCRP and IL-6 provide independent and/or complementary prognostic information for MACE and all-cause death after recent ACS.

Key results. Among 11,817 post-ACS patients, hsCRP independently predicted MACE after adjustment for IL-6 and clinical covariates, whereas IL-6 did not; both biomarkers independently predicted all-cause death, and having both markers elevated conferred highest risk.

Clinical relevance. hsCRP remains a robust inflammatory risk marker after ACS for predicting recurrent ischemic events; combined biomarker assessment may better identify patients at high mortality risk who might benefit from intensified secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Clarifies relative prognostic value of widely available inflammatory markers and supports targeted use of inflammatory risk stratification in post-ACS care and trials of anti-inflammatory therapies.

Cardiac Myosin-Binding Protein C for Diagnosis of Myocardial Infarction in Patients With Prior Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-10-10 · Prospective multicenter diagnostic comparative study

Odermatt R, Koechlin L, Lopez-Ayala P, Kaplan E, Recuenco LH, Huré G, Bima P, Crisanti L, et al.

Objective. Compare diagnostic accuracy of cardiac myosin‑binding protein C (cMyC) versus high‑sensitivity cardiac troponins (hs‑cTnT/I) for myocardial infarction in patients with prior CABG presenting with chest pain.

Key results. In 4752 patients (387 with prior CABG), cMyC had high diagnostic accuracy (AUC 0.88) in the CABG subgroup but was not superior to hs‑cTnT (AUC 0.89) or hs‑cTnI (AUC 0.88).

Clinical relevance. cMyC is a reliable biomarker for MI diagnosis in post‑CABG patients but does not clearly outperform established high‑sensitivity troponins; routine replacement of troponin assays is not supported by these data.

Why this paper matters. New biomarkers must demonstrate incremental diagnostic benefit over hs‑troponins in challenging subgroups before changing practice; this study tempers expectations for cMyC in CABG patients.

Volume vs intensity of physical activity and risk of cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular chronic diseases.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 23 · Prospective cohort study (UK Biobank, device- and self-reported PA)

Wei J, Shen M, Li S, Xiao Y, Luo D, Ferrari G, Lee DH, Rezende LFM, et al.

Objective. Examine associations of the proportion of vigorous physical activity (%VPA) relative to total activity volume with incidence of MACE and other chronic diseases, and the relative importance of intensity vs volume.

Key results. In device-measured data, a higher %VPA (>4%) was associated with 29–61% lower risks of multiple chronic diseases including MACE independent of total activity volume; intensity contributed more than volume for several outcomes, including MACE.

Clinical relevance. Findings support emphasizing higher-intensity activity (when appropriate) in cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention exercise prescriptions to maximize cardiovascular risk reduction.

Why this paper matters. Provides large-cohort evidence that exercise intensity, not just volume, should be considered in designing rehabilitation programs and counseling patients after coronary events.

Real-Life Psychological Experiences and Cardiac Autonomic Physiology Among Survivors of a Myocardial Infarction in Midlife.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-10-16 · Prospective observational cohort with ecological momentary assessment and Holter monitoring

Vaccarino V, She H, Elon L, Lewis TT, Ko YA, Clifford G, Li Q, Murrah N, et al.

Objective. Characterize daily-life psychological stress/mood and concomitant cardiac autonomic physiology (deceleration capacity, HRV) in midlife survivors of myocardial infarction, with attention to sex differences.

Key results. Among 302 MI survivors ≤61 years, women reported higher negative mood and had lower parasympathetic activity (deceleration capacity and HRV) than men; higher daily negative mood was associated with larger reductions in deceleration capacity among women.

Clinical relevance. Highlights that midlife women post-MI exhibit greater parasympathetic withdrawal linked to negative mood, supporting integration of psychosocial assessment and targeted interventions into cardiac rehabilitation for women.

Why this paper matters. Identifies a modifiable psychophysiological pathway that may help explain poorer outcomes in younger women after MI and argues for psychosocially informed rehab strategies.

Patient Characteristics and Outcomes Associated With Home-Based Cardiac Rehabilitation.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-10-10 · Retrospective cohort study

Mefford MT, Zhou M, Zhou H, Harrison TN, Silliman ME, Shin E, Batiste CD, Lahti DA, et al.

Objective. Describe patterns of referral to and participation in home‑based cardiac rehabilitation (HBCR) and examine associations of referral/participation with hospitalization and mortality outcomes in a large integrated health system.

Key results. Among 44,468 eligible adults, 29.6% received HBCR referral; referral and participation were each associated with significantly lower 180‑ and 365‑day all‑cause and cardiovascular hospitalizations, but older adults and women were less likely to be referred.

Clinical relevance. Timely HBCR referral and participation are associated with reduced hospitalizations, supporting wider implementation and targeted efforts to address referral disparities to improve secondary prevention outcomes.

Why this paper matters. Provides strong health‑system‑level evidence that scaling HBCR and closing referral gaps can reduce rehospitalizations after qualifying cardiac events.

Safety of low lipoprotein(a) levels: the FOURIER trial.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 26 · Randomized controlled trial (post hoc analysis)

Gencer B, Giugliano RP, Ran X, Mach F, Stroes ESG, Gouni-Berthold I, Češka R, Ezhov MV, et al.

Objective. Assess safety associations of low lipoprotein(a) (Lp(a)) concentrations and whether evolocumab (PCSK9 inhibitor) affects diabetes risk in patients with stable ASCVD enrolled in FOURIER.

Key results. In 25,090 participants, low Lp(a) was not associated with haemorrhagic stroke, serious bleeding, neurocognitive events, malignancy, or atrial fibrillation, but lower baseline Lp(a) was modestly associated with higher prevalent and incident diabetes. Evolocumab did not increase diabetes risk irrespective of baseline Lp(a).

Clinical relevance. Findings are reassuring about most safety endpoints for therapies that markedly lower Lp(a), but they highlight a small association between low Lp(a) and diabetes risk that merits glycaemic monitoring during Lp(a)-lowering interventions.

Why this paper matters. As Lp(a)-lowering agents advance to outcome trials, this analysis informs safety surveillance priorities and counseling for patients with established ASCVD.

Lipoprotein(a) and residual cardiovascular risks in Japanese patients with coronary artery disease who achieve guideline-recommended low-density lipoprotein cholesterol goals.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 26 · Multicenter retrospective observational study

Kataoka Y, Ray KK, Nicholls SJ, Katasako-Yabumoto A, Kitahara S, Hosoda H, Mitsui K, Murai K, et al.

Objective. Evaluate whether elevated Lp(a) predicts residual risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) among Japanese patients with CAD who achieved guideline LDL-C (<55 mg/dL) after PCI.

Key results. In 1,581 patients, elevated Lp(a) remained associated with higher MACE rates even when LDL-C was <55 mg/dL; standardized 5‑year MACE was substantially greater with Lp(a) ≥30 mg/dL and ≥50 mg/dL, and an Lp(a) threshold of ≈28.2 mg/dL was identified.

Clinical relevance. Lp(a) identifies residual risk despite aggressive LDL lowering and supports measuring Lp(a) in secondary prevention to identify patients who may need additional risk-modifying strategies or enrollment in Lp(a)-targeted trials.

Why this paper matters. Provides real-world evidence that Lp(a) contributes to recurrent event risk in treated CAD, with implications for testing thresholds and triage for emerging Lp(a)-lowering therapies.

Lipoprotein apheresis in the era of new lipid-lowering therapies.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 26 · Review

Sbrana F, Dal Pino B, Schettler VJJ, Muso E, Harada-Shiba M, Di Mola M, Kagitani M, Pineda E, et al.

Objective. Summarize current role, benefits, and limitations of lipoprotein apheresis (LA) in the context of newer lipid-lowering pharmacotherapies.

Key results. LA selectively removes apoB100-containing lipoproteins including LDL-C and Lp(a), has documented vascular benefits in severe dyslipidaemia (notably HoFH and high Lp(a)), and remains a safe option despite expanding pharmacologic options; access and underdiagnosis are major barriers.

Clinical relevance. LA remains a therapeutic option for patients with refractory severe hyperlipidaemia or substantially elevated Lp(a) in secondary prevention when pharmacotherapy is insufficient or unavailable.

Why this paper matters. Provides a contemporary framework for where LA fits alongside novel agents and highlights gaps (diagnosis, access) relevant to secondary prevention strategies.

Predicting lifetime cardiovascular risk and benefits of preventive treatment in patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease: the SMART-REACH2 model.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 23 · Multicohort model derivation and large-scale external validation (SMART-REACH2)

Holtrop J, Gynnild MN, Deo SV, Lim CE, Logothetis SB, Läll K, Oksanen M, Dalen H, et al.

Objective. Update and recalibrate the SMART-REACH lifetime risk model to estimate recurrent CV event risk and lifetime benefit of intensified preventive treatments across geographic risk regions in patients with established ASCVD.

Key results. Derived in 8,708 patients and externally validated in >2 million patients, SMART-REACH2 had pooled C-statistic ~0.68 with adequate calibration; it estimates lifetime gains in CV disease-free life expectancy (e.g., 2–4.4 years for a 50‑year-old with intensified BP and LDL reductions) depending on risk region.

Clinical relevance. The model provides individualized short-term and lifetime risk and treatment benefit estimates to inform shared decision-making and tailoring of secondary prevention intensity.

Why this paper matters. Offers a scalable, regionally recalibrated decision tool to quantify expected benefit from intensified preventive measures in patients with prior ASCVD.

Global Prevalence of Adherence to Antihypertensives and Associated Moderating Factors Among Adults With Hypertension: A Meta-Analysis.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-10-16 · Systematic review and meta-analysis

Ngolombe R, Chiona BC, Khangamwa Singano F, Sinyiza F, Chilinda ZB, Wang TJ

Objective. Estimate global prevalence of adherence to antihypertensive medications among adults with hypertension and explore moderators of adherence.

Key results. Pooled global adherence prevalence was 58% (95% CI 52–63%), with regional variation (Europe 67%, North America 45%); proportions of high, moderate, and low adherence indicate that many patients have suboptimal adherence, with age and assessment tool influencing estimates.

Clinical relevance. Suboptimal antihypertensive adherence is common and undermines secondary prevention efforts; systems-level and patient-level interventions are needed to improve blood pressure control.

Why this paper matters. Quantifies the global scope of adherence gaps relevant to preventing recurrent cardiovascular events and informs priorities for adherence-improvement strategies in secondary prevention programs.

Antithrombotic Therapy for Secondary Stroke Prevention in Patients With Symptomatic Large-Artery Atherosclerosis and Atrial Fibrillation: A Multicenter Experience.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-10-16 · Retrospective multicenter observational study

Mohamed GA, de Havenon A, Martin RH, Nahab F, Kasshout T, Qdais AA, Hanna M, Myesrlis EP, et al.

Objective. Describe real-world antithrombotic prescribing patterns and associated short-term outcomes in patients with atrial fibrillation who had ischemic stroke due to symptomatic large‑artery atherosclerosis.

Key results. Among 191 patients, regimens varied widely; single anticoagulant+single antiplatelet was most common and associated with lower observed short-term stroke/death than anticoagulant alone, though bleeding rates were substantial; many patients changed regimen within a year.

Clinical relevance. Highlights heterogeneous practice and the tradeoff between ischemic protection and bleeding in a high‑risk secondary prevention subgroup, underscoring the need for prospective trials to define optimal regimen and duration.

Why this paper matters. Provides real-world outcome data to inform risk–benefit discussions and trial design for antithrombotic strategies after stroke with concomitant large-artery disease and AF.

Residual Cardiovascular Risk Beyond Coronary Physiology: Complementary Prognostic Value of Trimethylamine N-Oxide in Patients Undergoing Coronary Computed Tomography Angiography With Computed Tomography-Derived Fractional Flow Reserve.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-10-16 · Biobank-linked retrospective cohort study

Shao B, Zhang R, Guo J, Li X, Liu GA, Liu F

Objective. Assess whether circulating trimethylamine N‑oxide (TMAO) provides incremental prognostic information beyond CT-derived FFR (CT-FFR) in patients undergoing coronary CT angiography.

Key results. In ~3,978 patients, higher TMAO independently predicted major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events after adjustment for clinical variables and CT-FFR and modestly improved discrimination; among patients with preserved CT-FFR, elevated TMAO identified higher-risk individuals.

Clinical relevance. Integration of systemic biomarkers like TMAO with noninvasive physiological imaging may refine risk stratification to guide secondary prevention intensity beyond anatomical/physiological lesion assessment alone.

Why this paper matters. Suggests a path toward combined biomarker–imaging risk models to personalize preventive strategies in patients evaluated for coronary disease.

Effectiveness of Discrete Strategies and Implementation Bundles for Improving Clinician Prescription of Clinical Practice Guideline-Recommended Medications for Atherosclerotic Occlusive Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-10-16 · Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

Kirkham AM, Candeliere J, Presseau J, McIsaac DI, Shorr R, Fergusson DA, Roberts DJ

Objective. Evaluate effectiveness of discrete implementation strategies and bundled interventions to improve clinician prescription of guideline-recommended medications for atherosclerotic occlusive disease and downstream patient outcomes.

Key results. Among 31 RCTs, the largest improvement in guideline medication prescription came from a bundle combining case management, team changes, continuous quality improvement, audit/feedback, and clinician/patient education (RR 2.62); team changes also reduced MACE risk.

Clinical relevance. Multifaceted implementation bundles and team-based care reliably increase uptake of evidence-based secondary prevention therapies and can improve clinical outcomes.

Why this paper matters. Provides actionable evidence for health systems seeking to close treatment gaps and improve secondary prevention delivery.

Lipoprotein(a) and Coronary Artery Disease Polygenic Risk Scores Do Not Improve Secondary Manifestations of Arterial Disease Score Prediction of Coronary Events After Ischemic Stroke.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-10-10 · Observational cohort study (UK Biobank)

Muthalaly RG, Tan S, Giovannucci J, Nerlekar N, Lin A, Nelson AJ, Phan T, Ma H, et al.

Objective. Test whether adding lipoprotein(a) and a coronary artery disease polygenic risk score improves prediction of incident coronary events beyond the SMART secondary prevention score among ischemic stroke survivors without baseline CAD.

Key results. Among 1,202 stroke survivors, SMART alone had moderate discrimination (AUC 0.69) and adding Lp(a), CAD‑PRS, or both did not meaningfully improve discrimination, Brier score, or reclassification.

Clinical relevance. Routine incorporation of Lp(a) or CAD polygenic risk scores for coronary risk stratification after ischemic stroke is not supported by this analysis.

Why this paper matters. Helps prioritize limited testing resources and suggests that established clinical risk scores remain the mainstay for coronary risk prediction in stroke survivors.

Prognostic Value of Coronary Computed Tomography Angiography-Derived Fractional Flow Reserve From a Large Nationwide Cohort.

Circulation · 2026-09-22 · Nationwide observational cohort study

Bell JS, Nicol E, Venugopal V, Mullen L, Tidbury L, Shaw M, Rogers C, Mullen S, et al.

Objective. Evaluate prognostic associations between stenosis‑specific and distal-vessel FFR‑CT values and individual outcomes (MI, cardiovascular death, all‑cause death, revascularization) in stable CAD across a large nationwide cohort.

Key results. In 7,836 patients, lower FFR‑CT values were incrementally associated with higher risk of MI, cardiovascular death, all‑cause death, and revascularization; FFR‑CT≤0.7 independently predicted MI and an optimal risk cutoff of ~0.67 was identified, with modest improvement in discrimination.

Clinical relevance. FFR‑CT adds prognostic information beyond stenosis severity and risk factors and can inform risk stratification and management decisions in stable CAD secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Large-scale real‑world evidence supports using noninvasive physiological assessment to guide preventive and revascularization strategies.
