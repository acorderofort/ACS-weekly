Severe obesity in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarctions: association with age, temporal trends, and clinical outcomes.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Oct 13 · Single-centre observational cohort (consecutive STEMI admissions)

Schmucker J, Kerniss H, Hanses U, Rühle S, Marín LAM, Osteresch R, Fach A, Eitel I, et al.

Objective. To determine prevalence, temporal trends, and outcomes associated with severe obesity (BMI ≥40) in patients presenting with STEMI between 2006 and 2022.

Key results. Severe obesity prevalence rose over time (1.9% to 2.6%), patients were younger and more often diabetic; severe obesity was linked to higher 30-day and 5-year mortality (adjusted HRs ~2.0) despite similar in-hospital adverse event rates and treatments.

Clinical relevance. Highlights a growing high-risk STEMI subgroup (severely obese, younger patients) for whom aggressive primary and secondary prevention efforts and long-term follow-up are warranted.

Why this paper matters. Signals changing epidemiology of STEMI with rising severe obesity and worse longer-term prognosis, underscoring prevention and tailored post-MI care but does not alter acute reperfusion strategies.

Intraprocedural Cardiac Arrest in Patients Undergoing Percutaneous Coronary Interventions in the United States: An Analysis of the NCDR CathPCI Registry.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026 Oct 12 · Large registry analysis (NCDR CathPCI Registry, 2018–2023)

Mosarla RC, Shah B, Rao SV, Bangalore S, Manandhar P, Brothers L, Rymer J, Smilowitz NR

Objective. To quantify contemporary incidence, trends, and predictors of intraprocedural cardiac arrest (IPCA) during PCI and to describe associated in-hospital mortality.

Key results. IPCA occurred in 0.93% of PCIs (higher in ACS: 1.28%), incidence rose from 0.62% (2018) to 1.07% (2023); in-hospital mortality for IPCA was 40.5% with modest declines in risk-adjusted mortality over time; multiple clinical/procedural predictors were identified (e.g., STEMI, shock, ventricular arrhythmia, complications).

Clinical relevance. Provides contemporary benchmarks for procedural risk during PCI, identifies high-risk presentations and complications that should prompt readiness for resuscitation and advanced support during ACS interventions.

Why this paper matters. High mortality associated with IPCA underlines need for preparedness and may inform quality-improvement, team training, and resource allocation in cath labs managing ACS.

Immediate Versus Staged Complete Revascularization in Multivessel Disease and STEMI: A Meta-Analysis of Randomized Trials.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026 Oct 12 · Meta-analysis of randomized trials

O'Callaghan D, Rai H, Giacoppo D, Coughlan JJ, Colleran R, Durand R, Spence MS, Kennedy M, et al.

Objective. To compare immediate versus staged complete revascularization of nonculprit lesions in patients with STEMI and multivessel disease, focusing on all-cause mortality and ischemic outcomes at ≥1 year.

Key results. In pooled data from 4 RCTs (2,651 patients), immediate complete revascularization was associated with higher 1-year all-cause mortality (HR 1.50) but reduced recurrent MI when procedural MI definitions were included; no difference in cardiac death or unplanned revascularization; results are fragile given low event counts.

Clinical relevance. Findings caution against routine immediate nonculprit PCI in STEMI and support staged strategies for many patients, potentially influencing timing decisions in multivessel STEMI revascularization.

Why this paper matters. Directly informs an important therapeutic decision in STEMI care; suggests staged revascularization may be safer for mortality although uncertainty remains and further trials are needed.

Frequency and Prognosis of Alternative Definitions of Occlusion Myocardial Infarction Across the Whole Spectrum of Acute Coronary Syndromes.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026 Oct 12 · Comparative prognostic analysis within a multicentre trial (MATRIX)

Milzi A, Zito A, Landi A, Frigoli E, Andò G, Boccuzzi G, Calabrò P, Campo G, et al.

Objective. To evaluate frequency and prognostic implications of five proposed definitions of occlusion myocardial infarction (OMI) across the ACS spectrum and compare them with conventional STE/NSTE-ACS classification.

Key results. OMI frequency varied by definition (37.8%–76.1%); definitions based on impaired culprit artery TIMI flow (0–2) were independently associated with higher 1-year mortality and improved risk discrimination beyond STE/NSTE-ACS; the broadest definition including troponin thresholds did not predict mortality.

Clinical relevance. Identifying OMI by impaired culprit flow can unmask high-risk patients without ST elevation who have mortality similar to STEMI and may warrant earlier invasive management and revascularization strategies.

Why this paper matters. Challenges reliance on ST-segment criteria alone to triage patients and supports an occlusion-focused diagnostic framework that could improve early identification of patients who need urgent reperfusion.

Occlusion-Focused Triage vs Very-High-Risk NSTEMI Criteria: A DIFOCCULT-3 Substudy.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026 Oct 12 · Cluster-randomized multicentre substudy (DIFOCCULT-3)

Aslanger EK, Aggül B, Yıldırımtürk Ö, Karabay CY, Meyers HP, Smith SW, Değertekin M, DIFOCCULT-3 Study Investigators

Objective. To compare an occlusion-focused (OMI/NOMI) diagnostic and triage strategy versus guideline very-high-risk NSTEMI criteria for identifying acute coronary occlusion (ACO) and directing early invasive management in patients without diagnostic STE.

Key results. OMI/NOMI approach selected far more patients for immediate invasive management (45.4% vs 13.6%) and identified adjudicated ACO within 2 hours more frequently (35.1% vs 16.7%) without reducing angiographic yield.

Clinical relevance. An occlusion-focused triage identifies more patients without ST elevation who have ACO and may expedite reperfusion; this has direct implications for triage protocols and cath-lab activation strategies in NSTEMI presentations.

Why this paper matters. Provides randomized-cluster evidence that an OMI-centered paradigm can increase early detection and treatment of ACO compared with current very-high-risk NSTEMI criteria, potentially altering diagnostic pathways.

Acute Coronary Occlusions in NSTEMI Undergoing Percutaneous Coronary Intervention: Insights From the CathPCI Registry.

JACC. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026 Oct 12 · Large observational registry analysis (CathPCI Registry, 2018–2023)

Kherallah RY, Zaid S, Assaf Y, Marks AC, Levine G, Kleiman NS, Khalid MU, Mascarenhas L, et al.

Objective. To determine the incidence, characteristics, and in-hospital outcomes of acute coronary occlusions (ACOs) among patients undergoing PCI for STEMI and NSTEMI and compare outcomes across STEMI, NSTEMI-ACO, and NSTEMI-no-ACO groups.

Key results. Among ~1.2M PCIs, NSTEMI-ACO comprised ~11% of cases (and ~24% when including TIMI 0–1) and had substantially worse in-hospital outcomes than NSTEMI-no-ACO but better outcomes than STEMI; NSTEMI-ACO had higher rates of shock, ventricular arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest.

Clinical relevance. Demonstrates that ACO is common in NSTEMI and confers markedly higher in-hospital risk, supporting need for earlier recognition and possibly more aggressive early invasive strategies in selected NSTEMI patients.

Why this paper matters. Large-scale evidence that many high-risk occlusions present without ST elevation and are associated with adverse outcomes, reinforcing occlusion-focused triage and reconsideration of timing thresholds for urgent PCI in NSTEMI.

Body composition changes in patients on GLP-1 receptor agonists undergoing an intensive cardiac rehabilitation program.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Oct 13 · Retrospective cohort study (intensive cardiac rehabilitation programme)

Jennings I, Ly K, Marooki M, Dzotsi M, McLaren G, Cuomo RE, Abohashem S, Taub PR

Objective. To determine whether GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) use during an intensive cardiac rehabilitation (ICR) programme is associated with changes in skeletal muscle mass (SMM) and fat mass (FM).

Key results. Among 468 ICR participants (67 on GLP-1 RAs), GLP-1 RA use was not associated with a significant difference in percent change in SMM or FM after adjustment for covariates; both groups improved fitness (METs).

Clinical relevance. Supports concurrent use of GLP-1 RAs with structured cardiac rehabilitation without clear signal for clinically relevant muscle loss in this cohort, informing medication-exercise integration decisions.

Why this paper matters. Addresses clinician concern about sarcopenia during pharmacologic weight loss in cardiac rehabilitation patients and supports combined therapy, though randomized data are lacking.

Association of body mass index on 10-year outcomes in patients treated with percutaneous coronary intervention and drug-eluting stents (the DECADE cooperation).

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Oct 13 · Pooled analysis of five randomized trials with 10-year follow-up

Scalamogna M, Simonetti F, Coughlan JJ, Ortega-Paz L, Räber L, Jensen LO, Maeng M, Brugaletta S, et al.

Objective. To assess the association between baseline BMI categories and 10-year outcomes (all-cause and cardiovascular death, MI, stent thrombosis, repeat revascularization) after PCI with drug-eluting stents.

Key results. Underweight patients had higher 10-year all-cause mortality versus normal weight (adjusted HR 1.58); overweight patients had lower all-cause and CV death risk, with a U-shaped association between BMI and mortality; obese patients showed mixed findings including higher early stent thrombosis risk.

Clinical relevance. BMI at time of PCI may inform long-term risk stratification for secondary prevention, but BMI alone is an imperfect marker and findings do not directly change immediate post-PCI management.

Why this paper matters. Reinforces complexity of 'obesity paradox' in post-PCI populations and the need for nuanced long-term secondary prevention strategies tailored to nutritional/body-composition status.

Visceral adipose tissue is associated with recurrent cardiovascular events in premature coronary artery disease: sub-analysis of the GEA study cohort.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026 Oct 13 · Retrospective sub-analysis of a multicentre cohort (GEA study)

Medina-Urrutia AX, Antonio-Villa NE, Martínez-Sánchez FD, Posadas-Sánchez R, Bello-Chavolla OY, Martínez-Alvarado MDR, Jorge-Galarza E, Juárez-Rojas JG

Objective. To investigate whether baseline visceral adipose tissue (VAT) quantified by CT predicts recurrent major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) over ~5 years in patients with premature coronary artery disease (pCAD).

Key results. Patients in the highest VAT tertile (≥194 cm²) had significantly higher adjusted risk of recurrent MACE (aHR 2.71) and non-fatal MACE (aHR 3.58); fatal MACE association was not statistically significant.

Clinical relevance. VAT measured by imaging identifies residual risk in pCAD patients despite pharmacotherapy and could guide intensified lifestyle or targeted interventions for secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Identifies visceral fat as a potentially modifiable residual risk marker in premature CAD, supporting integration of body-fat–focused strategies into secondary prevention programs.
