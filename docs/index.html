Pathology of Genetic Variants in Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection.

Circulation · 2026-09-01 · Autopsy registry histopathologic and whole-exome sequencing study

Tanaka T, Kawakami R, Gaynor BJ, Williams D, Augenstreich J, Sakamoto A, Jinnouchi H, Kawai K, et al.

Objective. To determine how pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in candidate SCAD genes relate to histopathological and morphological properties of human spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD) lesions.

Key results. Culprit SCAD lesions had reduced medial collagen and decreased smoothelin staining with smooth muscle cell phenotypic modulation. Pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants were found in 25% of cases and were associated with more frequent multivessel dissection; variants in COL3A1, FBN1, and FLNA showed reduced corresponding protein expression in the arterial media.

Clinical relevance. Provides pathological and genetic evidence that extracellular matrix and smooth muscle cell abnormalities contribute to SCAD susceptibility and more severe presentations, supporting consideration of genetic evaluation in selected SCAD patients.

Why this paper matters. Improves mechanistic understanding of SCAD-related myocardial infarction and may inform risk stratification, family screening, and the development of targeted diagnostics or therapies to prevent recurrent ACS in this subgroup.

Metrnl Reduces Thrombosis and Acute Myocardial Injury Through Inhibition of Platelet Activation via CD36 Receptor Degradation.

Circulation · 2026-08-18 · Preclinical mechanistic (mouse models, platelet assays, myocardial ischemia/reperfusion)

Miao ZW, Chen CX, Wang P, Zheng SL, Jiang JB, Hu WJ, Zhu XY, Lu B, et al.

Objective. To investigate the role of Metrnl (Meteorin-like) on platelet activation, thrombosis, and acute myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury and to define the receptor-mediated mechanism.

Key results. Metrnl inhibited platelet activation and thrombosis without increasing bleeding, acted via binding to platelet CD36 and promoted CD36 degradation (acetylation at K403) with downstream suppression of SRC–ERK5 signaling, and reduced microvascular thrombosis and myocardial injury in mouse ischemia/reperfusion models.

Clinical relevance. Identifies the Metrnl–CD36 axis as a novel antiplatelet/antithrombotic mechanism that could be harnessed to limit thrombotic complications and reperfusion injury in acute myocardial infarction.

Why this paper matters. Offers a potential new therapeutic approach to reduce thrombosis and myocardial injury in ACS, but findings are preclinical and require clinical translation and safety evaluation.

Great debate: preventive percutaneous coronary intervention added to optimal medical treatment should be the default treatment for non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques.

European heart journal · 2026 Oct 05 · Review/Opinion (Great Debate)

Park DW, Boden WE, De Caterina R, Gallinoro E, Ahn JM, Bairey Merz CN, Kaski JC, Kang DY, et al.

Objective. To examine the evidence for and against routine preventive PCI of non-flow-limiting vulnerable plaques compared with optimal medical therapy.

Key results. Contemporary intracoronary imaging can identify vulnerable plaques and the PREVENT trial showed reductions in a composite outcome driven mainly by softer endpoints in an open-label design, with no clear mortality or hard-endpoint benefit; procedural risks, cost-effectiveness, and the impact of modern lipid therapies remain unresolved.

Clinical relevance. Summarizes current uncertainties and cautions about adopting preventive PCI as routine secondary prevention, emphasizing the need for robust randomized data with hard clinical endpoints and consideration of intensive medical therapy.

Why this paper matters. Directly informs interventional and secondary prevention strategies aimed at preventing future ACS from non-flow-limiting lesions and underscores gaps in evidence guiding practice change.

The benefit of LDL-C levels below 55 mg/dL in ESKD patients under statin treatment: A target trial emulation study.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-08-25 · Target-trial emulation observational cohort study (ESKD hemodialysis patients on statins)

Yen CL, Fan PC, Chen JJ, Lee CC, Ho WY, Tu YR, Chu PH, Hsiao CC, et al.

Objective. To evaluate whether achieving LDL-C <55 mg/dL is associated with improved cardiovascular outcomes in end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) patients receiving statin therapy.

Key results. In this emulated target trial of 3,431 statin-treated hemodialysis patients, LDL-C <55 mg/dL was associated with lower risk of cardiovascular death versus LDL-C ≥100 mg/dL (HR 0.71; 95% CI 0.53–0.95), with a non-significant trend toward reduced all-cause mortality.

Clinical relevance. Suggests that very low achieved LDL-C may reduce cardiovascular mortality in a high-risk ESKD population and supports evaluation of lower LDL targets in dedicated randomized trials for these patients.

Why this paper matters. Addresses an important gap since prior RCTs in ESKD were neutral; if confirmed, findings could change secondary prevention LDL targets in dialysis populations.

Discordant fatty acid profile drives residual inflammatory risk in metabolically vulnerable patients with low atherogenic lipoprotein burden.

Atherosclerosis · 2026-08-24 · Multi-cohort observational study with discovery (GC-MS), OCT phenotyping, and UK Biobank validation

Wang Y, Han C, Yao J, Feng Y, Qiao S, Liu C, Liu Z, Ren F, et al.

Objective. To determine whether latent fatty-acid phenotypes predict structural plaque vulnerability and major adverse cardiovascular events independent of atherogenic lipoprotein burden.

Key results. An omega-3–depleted phenotype (Phenotype B) showed thinner fibrous caps and higher thin-cap fibroatheroma prevalence on OCT despite lower LDL-C, and in UK Biobank was associated with increased MACE (adjusted HR 1.12), with much of the excess risk mediated via glycemic, inflammatory, and adiposity pathways.

Clinical relevance. Identifies a fatty-acid biomarker profile that may explain residual cardiovascular risk despite optimized lipoproteins and could guide adjunctive preventive strategies beyond LDL lowering.

Why this paper matters. Highlights non-lipoprotein metabolic pathways (omega-3 depletion, inflammation, adiposity, glycemia) as actionable targets to reduce recurrent ACS and other events in patients already treated for atherogenic lipoproteins.
