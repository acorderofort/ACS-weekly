Angiography-Derived vs Wire-Based Fractional Flow Reserve to Guide Revascularization: The Randomized LIPSIA-STRATEGY Trial.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-11-01 · Multicenter randomized trial

Majunke N, Desch S, Kister T, Buske M, Pöss J, Feistritzer HJ, Erbs S, Hösler N, et al.

Objective. Compare angiography-derived vessel FFR (vFFR) versus pressure-wire FFR to guide revascularization for intermediate coronary stenoses in patients with acute or chronic coronary syndromes.

Key results. In 1,053 randomized patients, 1‑year major adverse cardiac events were 3.0% (vFFR) vs 4.1% (FFR); noninferiority by absolute margin met (risk difference −1.01 pp; P for noninferiority <0.001), though noninferiority was not met using a post hoc relative margin in the primary mITT population.

Clinical relevance. Supports vFFR as a wire-free alternative for physiological guidance of revascularization in intermediate lesions, potentially expanding access to physiology-guided decision making in ACS settings.

Why this paper matters. Randomized evidence that a noninvasive angiography-derived approach can approximate wire-based FFR outcomes may influence procedural workflow and adoption, but caveats about sensitivity to noninferiority margins and additional revascularization remain.

Nonculprit Lesion Complexity and Timing of Complete Revascularization in Patients Presenting With Acute Coronary Syndrome and Multivessel Disease: Insights From the BIOVASC Trial.

Circulation. Cardiovascular interventions · 2026-11-13 · Post hoc exploratory analysis of randomized BIOVASC trial

Scherillo G, Elscot JJ, Bennett J, Sabaté M, Esposito G, Boersma E, den Dekker WK, Van Mieghem NM, et al.

Objective. Compare immediate versus staged complete revascularization in ACS patients with multivessel disease stratified by nonculprit lesion (NCL) complexity.

Key results. Among 1,525 BIOVASC patients, 36.1% had complex NCLs which were associated with higher 2-year rates of the primary composite end point (adjusted HR 1.49); no significant interaction between NCL complexity and timing strategy was observed.

Clinical relevance. Nonculprit lesion complexity identifies higher-risk ACS patients, but optimal timing of complete revascularization (immediate vs staged) did not differ materially in this analysis.

Why this paper matters. Helps clinicians weigh lesion complexity when planning multivessel PCI in ACS, suggesting complexity predicts worse outcomes though timing strategy may be individualized.

Temporal Changes in Lipoprotein(a) Following Acute Coronary Syndrome: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

European journal of preventive cardiology · 2026-11-11 · Systematic review and meta-analysis

Shokravi A, Sethb J, Yoonb J, Mancini GBJ, Turgeon RD, Brunham LR, Fordyce CB

Objective. Characterize temporal changes in lipoprotein(a) after acute coronary syndrome (ACS).

Key results. Pooled data from 16 studies (1,396 patients) show dynamic Lp(a) trajectory with significant increases at 7–14 days (+44%), 1 month (+28%) and 3–6 months (+22%) versus initial post‑ACS measurements; early measurements may underestimate later levels.

Clinical relevance. Timing of Lp(a) measurement after ACS matters for clinical decision thresholds and eligibility for emerging Lp(a)-lowering therapies.

Why this paper matters. Supports repeat Lp(a) testing weeks to months after ACS when initial values are near treatment thresholds to avoid misclassification for secondary prevention strategies.

Percutaneous Coronary Intervention in Dialysis Patients: Insights from a Nationwide Registry Analysis.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-11-10 · Large registry analysis (CathPCI Registry)

Kuno T, Hiruma Y, Orui H, Ueyama HA, Fujisaki T, Aikawa T, Yeh RW, Poulin MF, et al.

Objective. Compare trans‑radial intervention (TRI) versus trans‑femoral intervention (TFI) for PCI in patients on dialysis.

Key results. Among 137,702 dialysis patients, TRI (28.3%) was associated with modestly lower access-site and overall bleeding, MI, and mortality but a higher stroke risk (driven by ischemic stroke).

Clinical relevance. Suggests potential bleeding and mortality benefits of TRI in dialysis patients undergoing PCI but raises concern about ischemic stroke trade-offs that require further study.

Why this paper matters. Informs access-site choice for a high‑risk, under‑studied population where preserving upper-limb vessels is often prioritized over bleeding risk.

Guideline-directed medical therapy use, persistence, and cardiovascular outcomes following ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction: a pooled analysis of three randomised trials with external registry validation.

The Lancet regional health. Europe · 2026-11-07 · Patient-level pooled analysis with registry validation

Tomii D, Heg D, Losdat S, Rohla M, Häner JD, Hunziker L, Fürholz M, Corpataux N, et al.

Objective. Assess implementation and long-term persistence of four-class guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) after STEMI and association with cardiovascular outcomes.

Key results. Among 2,270 trial patients and 5,643 registry patients, >2/3 received all four GDMT classes at discharge but proportions declined over 5 years; higher GDMT class at discharge and during follow-up was associated with lower 5-year risk of cardiovascular death, stroke, or MI in exploratory analyses.

Clinical relevance. Highlights substantial declines in long-term GDMT persistence after STEMI and an association between greater GDMT breadth and fewer events, underscoring opportunities to improve secondary prevention adherence.

Why this paper matters. Reinforces the importance of durable implementation of comprehensive medical therapy after MI and identifies a modifiable care gap linked to long-term outcomes.

First and Subsequent Major Cardiovascular Events With Oral Semaglutide in High-Risk Type 2 Diabetes: A Secondary Analysis of the SOUL Randomized Clinical Trial.

JAMA cardiology · 2026-11-11 · Secondary analysis of a randomized clinical trial (SOUL)

Marx N, Deanfield JE, Mulvagh SL, Videmark A, van Bemmel T, Buse JB, Davicevic-Elez Z, Engelmann MDM, et al.

Objective. Evaluate effect of oral semaglutide vs placebo on total (first and subsequent) 3‑point MACE in high-risk type 2 diabetes with ASCVD/CKD.

Key results. Among 9,650 participants, oral semaglutide reduced total 3‑point MACE events (683 vs 791 events; mean ratio 0.86; 95% CI 0.76–0.97; P=0.01), indicating reduction in total disease burden.

Clinical relevance. Extends SOUL trial findings by showing oral semaglutide lowers total cardiovascular event burden in high‑risk diabetes, informing secondary prevention pharmacotherapy choices.

Why this paper matters. Demonstrates an agent that reduces not only first events but subsequent events, relevant for long-term secondary prevention planning in patients with established ASCVD and diabetes.

Pelacarsen and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with High Lipoprotein(a).

The New England journal of medicine · 2026-11-07 · Large international randomized, placebo-controlled trial (NEJM)

Cho L, Nicholls SJ, Frndak S, Wolski K, Nordestgaard BG, Manning B, Leitersdorf E, Landmesser U, et al.

Objective. Determine whether pelacarsen, an antisense oligonucleotide that lowers Lp(a), reduces major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with preexisting CVD and high Lp(a).

Key results. In 8,322 efficacy‑population patients, pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) markedly at 12 months but did not reduce the primary composite MACE over median 4.7 years (12.3% pelacarsen vs 12.1% placebo; HR 1.02; P=0.76).

Clinical relevance. Negative cardiovascular outcome trial despite substantial Lp(a) lowering, directly impacts the therapeutic rationale for Lp(a)-targeted therapy in secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. High‑quality null result challenges the assumption that lowering Lp(a) with this agent reduces events and will influence ongoing development and patient selection for Lp(a)-directed therapies.

Interleukin-6 Inhibition with Ziltivekimab for Atherosclerotic Disease.

The New England journal of medicine · 2026-11-07 · Randomized, placebo-controlled trial (NEJM)

Ridker PM, Lincoff AM, Marx N, Navar AM, Sattar N, Tuttle KR, Budoff M, Böttcher M, et al.

Objective. Test whether interleukin‑6 inhibition with ziltivekimab reduces cardiovascular events in patients with ASCVD, CKD, and elevated hs‑CRP.

Key results. In 6,376 patients, ziltivekimab markedly reduced IL‑6 and hs‑CRP levels but did not reduce 3‑point MACE versus placebo over median 33 months (17.6% vs 17.7%; HR 0.99; P=0.84); serious infections were numerically higher.

Clinical relevance. High-quality negative trial for an inflammatory-targeted strategy in a high‑risk secondary prevention population.

Why this paper matters. Challenges IL‑6 neutralization as a viable approach to reduce cardiovascular events in this phenotype and will inform prioritization of anti‑inflammatory targets and trial design.

Assessment of Coronary Inflammatory Risk From Noncontrast Cardiac Computed Tomography.

JAMA cardiology · 2026-11-11 · Cohort study with AI algorithm development and external validation

Chan K, Gissler MC, Sobirov I, Wahome E, Tomlins P, Klüner LV, Oikonomou EK, Nicol E, et al.

Objective. Assess whether coronary inflammation measured by fat attenuation index (FAI) on noncontrast CT (NCCT) or CCTA improves cardiovascular risk prediction beyond coronary artery calcium (CAC) score.

Key results. In derivation and validation cohorts, elevated NCCT-derived FAI independently stratified CV mortality and MACE across CAC categories, notably among CAC=0; adding FAI improved AUC (0.76→0.80–0.81) and reclassification.

Clinical relevance. NCCT-derived FAI is a potential risk‑enhancing marker that may identify higher risk patients despite low CAC and inform intensification of secondary prevention.

Why this paper matters. Offers a widely available CT‑based inflammation metric that could refine residual risk assessment beyond calcified plaque burden in clinical practice.

Comparative Efficacy and Safety of Four Novel Monoclonal Antibody PCSK9 Inhibitors: A Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis of Lipid and Safety Outcomes.

Journal of the American Heart Association · 2026-11-10 · Bayesian network meta-analysis

Warraich K, Rasool MM, Farhan M, Murtaza M, Morales Ojeda LA, Khan F, Amjad H, Saleem MS, et al.

Objective. Compare efficacy and safety of four novel monoclonal antibody PCSK9 inhibitors versus placebo across lipid and safety outcomes.

Key results. Across 13 trials, all agents produced substantial LDL‑C reductions (−45% to −80%); some indirect between‑agent differences favored ongericimab, but certainty was moderate to very low and no safety signals beyond injection‑site reactions were detected.

Clinical relevance. Confirms strong LDL‑C lowering across new PCSK9 mAbs but underscores need for head‑to‑head and outcome trials before asserting a hierarchy for secondary prevention use.

Why this paper matters. Useful for clinicians and guideline developers assessing emerging lipid‑lowering options, but indirect comparisons are hypothesis‑generating rather than definitive.

Remnant Cholesterol and Atherosclerotic Risk in Chronic Kidney Disease: Meta-Analysis of Asian and Global Evidence.

JACC. Asia · 2026-10-14 · Systematic review and meta-analysis

Tripathi S, Jain D, Agarwal V

Objective. Determine whether higher remnant cholesterol is independently associated with atherosclerotic cardiovascular events in chronic kidney disease (CKD).

Key results. Pooled analysis of ~110,000 participants from 18 cohorts found higher remnant cholesterol associated with ASCVD (HR ~1.27–1.36 depending on scale), independent of LDL‑C/apolipoprotein B and consistent across regions (GRADE: moderate).

Clinical relevance. Identifies remnant cholesterol as an independent residual‑risk marker in CKD that could inform secondary prevention risk stratification and therapeutic hypotheses.

Why this paper matters. Supports attention to remnant cholesterol beyond LDL‑C in CKD patients, a population with high residual risk despite LDL‑directed therapy.

Impact of the 2026 Dyslipidemia Guideline on ASCVD Reclassification and Statin Recommendations in MESA.

JACC. Advances · 2026-11-13 · Cohort analysis (MESA)

Murphy BS, Hershey MS, Huang S, Nam Y, Post WS, Bertoni AG, Blumenthal RS, McClelland RL, et al.

Objective. Assess impact of the 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline (PREVENT equations and new thresholds) on ASCVD reclassification and statin recommendations.

Key results. In 6,098 MESA participants, PREVENT reclassified 16.4% downward and 13.1% upward versus PCE; statin recommendation fell modestly (50.4% vs 55.2%) with few subsequent events among those losing recommendation, while elevated CAC and risk enhancers identified higher‑risk reclassified individuals.

Clinical relevance. Transition to PREVENT may modestly reduce statin-eligible population but reclassification highlights individuals who may require additional risk‑enhancing evaluation (eg, CAC) for secondary prevention decisions.

Why this paper matters. Important for clinicians implementing the 2026 guideline to recognize net small changes in eligibility but meaningful individual reclassification that could affect secondary prevention strategies.

Left Atrial Appendage Closure in Patients Considered Unsuitable for Oral Anticoagulation: Randomized Outcomes Through 5 Years in ASAP-TOO.

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 2026-10-31 · Randomized trial (ASAP-TOO) with long-term follow-up

Reddy VY, Buchbinder M, Nair DG, Issa ZF, Betts TR, Schmidt B, Dukkipati SR, Bergmann MW, et al.

Objective. Evaluate long-term safety and effectiveness of left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) versus control (antiplatelet/no therapy) in AF patients deemed unsuitable for oral anticoagulation.

Key results. Trial stopped early (481 randomized); implantation success 97.8%; ischemic stroke/systemic embolism at up to 5 years was numerically lower with LAAC (7.8% vs 11.4%) but underpowered and not statistically conclusive; major bleeding was higher early with LAAC's antiplatelet regimen.

Clinical relevance. Provides exploratory long‑term data on LAAC in anticoagulation‑unsuitable AF patients but is underpowered to make definitive comparative effectiveness claims.

Why this paper matters. Adds important, though imprecise, evidence for shared decision‑making in high‑bleeding‑risk AF patients considering device‑based stroke prevention.
